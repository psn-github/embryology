## 1. Purpose

To define how the Embryology Laboratory keeps staff safe from the chemical and physical hazards of the workplace, and how information about those hazards is communicated through labels and safety data sheets. The policy ensures that every hazardous substance in the laboratory is identified, classified, labelled and understood by the people who use it.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all staff, trainees, contractors and visitors and to every chemical, compressed gas, cryogen and hazardous consumable used or stored in the laboratory — including culture media additives, fixatives, disinfectants, alcohols, liquid nitrogen and compressed CO₂ and other gases. Biological exposure is governed by the exposure-control policy (ELB-POL-001); chemical-handling procedure detail sits in chemical hygiene (ELB-SOP-014). Hazard communication in this suite follows the **Globally Harmonized System (GHS)**.

## 3. Policy statements

1. **Hazard inventory.** A current inventory of all hazardous substances is maintained, with the location, quantity and GHS classification of each. New substances are not introduced without being added to the inventory and risk-assessed.
2. **GHS labelling.** Every container of a hazardous substance carries a GHS-compliant label showing the product identifier, signal word, hazard and precautionary statements and pictograms. Substances decanted into secondary containers are labelled to the same standard; unlabelled containers are not used.
3. **Safety data sheets (SDS).** A current SDS is held and readily accessible for every hazardous substance. Staff know where the SDS collection is and consult it before first use of any substance and after any incident.
4. **Risk assessment and substitution.** Each hazardous task is risk-assessed; where a less hazardous substance can achieve the same result it is substituted. Embryotoxic and volatile compounds are minimised because of their effect on culture (air quality is monitored under ELB-SOP-013).
5. **Engineering controls and PPE.** Volatile and irritant chemicals are handled with appropriate ventilation or within a fume hood; PPE specified in the SDS and risk assessment is worn. Engineering controls take priority over PPE.
6. **Storage and segregation.** Hazardous substances are stored by compatibility (e.g. flammables, oxidisers and acids segregated), within rated cabinets, away from culture and clean areas. Compressed gases are secured and regulators are inspected.
7. **Spills and emergencies.** Spill response materials are available and staff are trained in their use. Emergency equipment — eyewash, spill kit, fire provision — is accessible, identified and checked on schedule.
8. **Training.** No member of staff handles a hazardous substance until trained in its GHS hazards, safe use, the location of its SDS and the emergency response.

## 4. Responsibilities

- **All staff** — read labels and SDS before use, apply the specified controls, and report spills, label damage or missing SDS.
- **Laboratory Lead** — maintains the hazard inventory and SDS collection, ensures correct labelling and storage, and arranges training and emergency provision.
- **Quality Manager** — audits the inventory, labelling and SDS currency, and tracks chemical incidents to closure.
- **Medical Director** — owns this policy and approves the locally defined controls and inventory limits in Appendix A.

## 6. Deviations

A missing or damaged GHS label, an absent or out-of-date SDS, an unassessed new substance, or a spill or chemical injury is recorded and managed under the deviation and CAPA policy (ELB-POL-006) using the deviation log (ELB-LOG-023). A chemical injury is also handled as an adverse event (ELB-POL-005). Use of an unlabelled hazardous substance is a stop condition.

## 7. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

Maximum on-site quantities of flammables and other hazard classes, storage-cabinet allocations, eyewash and spill-kit checking intervals, and the list of substances banned from the culture area are **locally defined** and recorded in **Appendix A** (flagged 🔧, requiring Medical Director sign-off). The classification, labelling and SDS framework is **GHS**; the wider safety-management requirement is **guideline-anchored** (ISO 15189; WHO biosafety guidance).

## 9. Teaching point

Hazard communication is a chain that only works end to end: the substance is classified, the label carries that classification in GHS pictograms and statements, the SDS expands it, and the person reads both before they open the bottle. In an embryology laboratory there is a second reason to care — volatile chemicals that are merely irritating to staff can be lethal to embryos, so the safest chemical is often the one you decide not to bring in at all.
