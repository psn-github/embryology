## 1. Purpose

To define how the Embryology Laboratory identifies, reports, investigates and learns from adverse events — incidents that have harmed, or could have harmed, a patient, their gametes or embryos, or staff. The policy ensures that serious events are surfaced honestly and quickly, investigated to their root cause, and used to prevent recurrence. It is implemented through the adverse-events and root-cause-analysis procedure (OMK-POL-EMB-0004).

## 2. Scope

Applies to all activities of the laboratory using the couple's own (autologous) gametes and embryos, and to events affecting patient material, patient safety or staff safety. Adverse events include loss or damage of gametes or embryos, misidentification or near-miss identity errors, storage failures, exposure incidents, and any laboratory event contributing to clinical harm. Lower-severity process departures without harm are handled as deviations (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005); the two policies are paired and an event may be both.

## 3. Policy statements

1. **Duty to report.** Every member of staff has a duty to report a suspected adverse event or near-miss promptly, without fear of blame. The laboratory operates a just, learning culture: reporting is expected and protected; concealment is not tolerated.
2. **Immediate safety first.** On recognising an event, the first action is to make the situation safe — protect the patient, secure and isolate the affected material, and prevent escalation — before investigation begins.
3. **Severity grading.** Each event is graded by actual and potential severity. The grading determines the speed and depth of response and whether external or clinical notification is required.
4. **Root-cause investigation.** Serious events are investigated by root-cause analysis (OMK-POL-EMB-0004), examining system and process factors rather than seeking individual blame. Witnessing and identification controls (OMK-SOP-EMB-0002, OMK-SOP-EMB-0007) are reviewed where identity is implicated.
5. **Corrective and preventive action.** Every confirmed adverse event generates corrective and preventive actions managed under OMK-SOP-EMB-0005, with owners and target dates, tracked to verified closure and effectiveness.
6. **Patient duty of candour.** Where a patient has been or may have been harmed, the treating clinician is informed promptly so the patient can be told openly, supported and offered next steps. The laboratory provides the factual account.
7. **Confidentiality and records.** Adverse-event records contain personal health information and are handled per the confidentiality policy (OMK-POL-EMB-0005). Investigation records are retained per the retention schedule.
8. **Trending and review.** Adverse events and near-misses are trended; recurring themes are escalated to management review so that systemic risk is addressed before it causes harm.

## 4. Responsibilities

- **All staff** — report events and near-misses promptly, make the situation safe, and support investigation honestly.
- **Laboratory Lead** — ensures immediate containment, initiates the adverse-event report and investigation (OMK-POL-EMB-0004), and assigns corrective actions.
- **Quality Manager** — maintains the adverse-event records, leads or coordinates root-cause analysis, tracks CAPA to closure, and reports trends.
- **Medical Director** — owns this policy, grades serious events, decides on clinical/external notification, and ensures duty of candour with the treating clinician.

## 6. Deviations

Failure to report an adverse event, late reporting beyond the local window, or failure to implement and verify the resulting corrective actions is itself a serious matter managed under the deviation and CAPA policy (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005) and logged (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005). Concealment of an event is a disciplinary matter and is escalated to the Medical Director.

## 7. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

The severity-grading scale, the reporting windows by grade, the criteria for external or clinical notification, and the CAPA effectiveness-verification interval are **locally defined** and recorded in **Appendix A** (flagged 🔧, requiring Medical Director sign-off). The principles of incident reporting, root-cause analysis and continual improvement are **guideline-anchored** (ESHRE 2015; ASRM 2022; ISO 15189).

## 9. Teaching point

The events worth learning from most are the ones that nearly happened. A near-miss is a free lesson — the system showed you where it can fail without anyone being harmed — and a laboratory that treats near-misses as seriously as harm is one that fixes problems while they are still cheap. Root-cause analysis asks "why did the system allow this," not "who did it," because the embryologist who reports an error is the safeguard, not the cause.
