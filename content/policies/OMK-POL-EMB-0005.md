## 1. Purpose

To define how the Embryology Laboratory protects the confidentiality of patient information and the privacy of patients undergoing fertility treatment. Fertility care is sensitive, and the laboratory holds identifying information, clinical detail, results and the location of stored gametes and embryos. This policy ensures that information is accessed only by those who need it, used only for the purpose for which it was given, and protected throughout its life.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all staff, trainees, contractors and visitors and to all patient information in any form — paper, electronic, witnessing-system, inventory and verbal — relating to couples whose own (autologous) gametes and embryos are handled. It covers collection, access, use, sharing, storage, retention and disposal of information, and the confidentiality of records held under related policies (consent OMK-POL-EMB-0006; cryostorage OMK-POL-EMB-0007). Confidentiality requirements are anchored to **ISO 15189**.

## 3. Policy statements

1. **Duty of confidence.** Every member of staff owes a duty of confidence to every patient. Patient information is not discussed, displayed or disclosed except as required to deliver care or as the patient has authorised. The duty continues after employment ends.
2. **Need-to-know access.** Access to patient information is granted on a least-privilege, need-to-know basis tied to the person's role. System access is individually authenticated; shared logins are not used, and access rights are reviewed and revoked promptly when roles change.
3. **Lawful, purpose-limited use.** Information is used only for the purpose for which it was provided — the couple's treatment, its quality assurance and statutory duties. It is not used for any unrelated purpose.
4. **Disclosure with authority.** Information is disclosed to third parties only with the patient's consent or where there is a clear lawful basis. For a couple, disclosure respects the privacy interests of both partners. Disclosures are recorded.
5. **Security of records.** Records are physically and electronically secured against loss, unauthorised access and alteration (OMK-SOP-EMB-0004). Identifying information is not left visible in shared areas; screens are locked when unattended; portable media and email containing patient data are protected.
6. **Identity linkage protected.** The links between a patient and their gametes, embryos and storage location (OMK-SOP-EMB-0007, OMK-SOP-EMB-0008, OMK-POL-EMB-0007) are themselves confidential and protected to the same standard as clinical records.
7. **Retention and disposal.** Records are retained for the defined retention period and then disposed of securely. Retention periods are recorded in Appendix A and applied consistently.
8. **Breach response.** A suspected confidentiality breach is reported immediately, contained, assessed for impact on the patient, and managed as a deviation (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005) and, where the patient is affected, an adverse event (OMK-POL-EMB-0004). Affected patients are informed where appropriate.
9. **Training and culture.** Confidentiality is covered at induction and reinforced; staff understand that curiosity about a record they are not caring for is itself a breach.

## 4. Responsibilities

- **All staff** — protect patient information, access only what their role requires, never discuss patients outside the care context, and report any suspected breach immediately.
- **Laboratory Lead** — ensures records and systems are secured, that access is least-privilege, and that staff are trained.
- **Quality Manager** — maintains access controls and the disclosure record, audits compliance and retention, and manages breach response to closure.
- **Medical Director** — owns this policy, decides on disclosures and breach notification, and approves the retention periods in Appendix A.

## 6. Deviations

A confidentiality breach, an inappropriate access, an unauthorised disclosure, or a failure of secure storage or disposal is recorded and managed under the deviation and CAPA policy (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005) using the deviation log (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005). A breach affecting a patient is escalated as an adverse event (OMK-POL-EMB-0004), and the patient is informed where appropriate.

## 7. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

The record-retention periods by record type, the access-authorisation matrix by role, the breach-reporting window, and the rules for transmitting patient data outside the laboratory are **locally defined** and recorded in **Appendix A** (flagged 🔧, requiring Medical Director sign-off). The duty of confidentiality, controlled access and protection of records is **guideline-anchored** (ISO 15189; ESHRE 2015).

## 9. Teaching point

Confidentiality in fertility care is not only a legal duty, it is part of the treatment: patients disclose the most private details on the trust that the laboratory will hold them quietly. The everyday risks are mundane — a screen left open, a corridor conversation, a curious look at a familiar name in the system. The simplest test is the most reliable one: do I need this information to do my job right now, and would the patient expect me to be looking.
