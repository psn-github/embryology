## 1. Purpose

To define how the Embryology Laboratory protects staff from occupational exposure to bloodborne and other potentially infectious material, and from the physical and cryogenic hazards inherent in gamete and embryo work. The policy sets the standard for engineering controls, work practices, personal protective equipment and the response to an exposure incident, so that no member of staff is harmed by the material they handle.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all staff, trainees, contractors and visitors who enter the laboratory or handle human gametes, embryos, semen, follicular fluid, blood-contaminated consumables, liquid nitrogen or cryogenic vapour. It covers exposure to potentially infectious human material and to the cryogenic and physical hazards of the cryostore. Chemical-specific hazards are managed under the occupational safety and hazard-communication policy (OMK-POL-EMB-0002) and chemical hygiene (OMK-POL-EMB-0002); latex sensitisation is managed under OMK-POL-EMB-0002. Patient material is autologous (the couple's own) throughout.

## 3. Policy statements

1. **Universal precautions.** All human biological material is treated as potentially infectious regardless of the patient's known serology. Hands are washed and gloves worn for every contact with gametes, embryos, semen or follicular fluid.
2. **Hierarchy of control.** Risk is controlled in order of effectiveness: elimination and substitution first, then engineering controls (laminar flow hoods, sharps containment, ventilation), then safe work practices, and personal protective equipment last. PPE supplements but never replaces engineering and work-practice controls.
3. **Personal protective equipment.** Appropriate gloves, laboratory coat and eye/face protection are worn for tasks with splash or aerosol potential. Cryogenic-rated gloves and a full face visor are worn when handling liquid nitrogen or open cryostorage (OMK-SOP-EMB-0045, OMK-SOP-EMB-0045). PPE is removed before leaving the laboratory and is not worn in clean office or rest areas.
4. **Sharps and aerosols.** Needles, pipette tips and glass are handled to minimise aerosol and percutaneous injury, are not recapped by hand, and are discarded directly into rigid sharps containers. Procedures with splash potential are performed within a hood.
5. **Cryogenic exposure.** Liquid nitrogen presents frostbite, asphyxiation and pressure hazards. Oxygen-depletion monitoring, ventilation and safe-handling controls for the cryostore are mandatory and detailed in the LN2 SOPs (OMK-SOP-EMB-0045, OMK-SOP-EMB-0045).
6. **Immunisation and health surveillance.** Staff with occupational exposure potential are offered hepatitis B immunisation and appropriate baseline and follow-up health surveillance, recorded confidentially.
7. **Exposure response.** Any percutaneous injury, mucosal splash or cryogenic injury is washed/irrigated immediately, reported the same day, assessed clinically, and recorded. A significant exposure is escalated as an adverse event (OMK-POL-EMB-0004) and reviewed as a deviation (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005).
8. **Decontamination and waste.** Surfaces and equipment are decontaminated after use and after any spill; biohazardous waste is segregated and disposed of per a retired/absorbed document.

## 4. Responsibilities

- **All staff** — apply universal precautions, use the provided controls and PPE correctly, and report any exposure or near-miss immediately.
- **Laboratory Lead** — ensures engineering controls and PPE are available and maintained, that hoods and the cryostore are serviced, and that staff are trained before unsupervised work.
- **Quality Manager** — maintains exposure and surveillance records, monitors incident trends, and verifies that corrective actions close out.
- **Medical Director** — owns this policy, approves the locally defined controls in Appendix A, and oversees the clinical management of any exposure.

## 6. Deviations

A failure of an engineering control, a missing or defective PPE item, or any exposure incident is recorded and managed under the deviation and CAPA policy (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005) using the deviation log (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005). A significant occupational exposure is also handled as an adverse event (OMK-POL-EMB-0004). Work that cannot be performed safely is stopped until the control is restored.

## 7. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

The PPE specification by task, the oxygen-depletion alarm setpoints for the cryostore, the decontamination agents and contact times, and the exposure-reporting window are **locally defined** and recorded in **Appendix A** (flagged 🔧, requiring Medical Director sign-off). The principles of universal precautions and the hierarchy of control are **guideline-anchored** (ISO 15189; WHO biosafety guidance) and hazard labelling follows **GHS**.

## 9. Teaching point

Exposure control works from the top of the hierarchy down: the safest laboratory is one where the hood, the sharps bin and the ventilation do the protecting, and PPE is the last layer rather than the first. The two hazards unique to embryology — infectious human material and cryogenic nitrogen — are both invisible until they injure you, which is exactly why the controls are engineered in advance and the response is rehearsed, not improvised.
