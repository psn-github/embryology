## 1. Purpose

To protect staff and patients from natural rubber latex (NRL) sensitisation and allergic reaction, and to protect gametes and embryos from latex-derived embryotoxicity, by controlling the use of latex-containing materials in the Embryology Laboratory. The policy sets a default toward latex-safe materials and defines how latex sensitivity is managed when it is known.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all staff, trainees, contractors, visitors and patients who enter or whose material is handled in the laboratory and theatre interface, and to all gloves, consumables and equipment that may contain natural rubber latex. It covers occupational sensitisation, the protection of latex-sensitive individuals, and the avoidance of latex contact with culture systems. PPE in general is covered under ELB-POL-001; hazard communication under ELB-POL-002.

## 3. Policy statements

1. **Latex-safe by default.** The laboratory operates as a low-latex environment. Powder-free, non-latex (e.g. nitrile) gloves are the standard choice for gamete and embryo handling, both to prevent sensitisation and to avoid latex/powder embryotoxicity.
2. **No latex in the culture pathway.** Materials that contact gametes, embryos, culture media or culture air are latex-free. Latex must never be introduced into the contact pathway of the culture system, and powdered gloves are prohibited near open culture.
3. **Procurement control.** Latex content is a documented criterion at procurement (ELB-SOP-007). Where a latex-free equivalent exists it is selected; any latex-containing item retained for a specific reason is identified and risk-assessed.
4. **Identification of sensitivity.** Known latex allergy or sensitisation — in a staff member or a patient — is identified, recorded confidentially, and flagged so that the individual's contact with latex is avoided in every interaction.
5. **Patient protection.** For a patient with declared latex allergy, the laboratory and the procedure interface use only latex-free materials, and the allergy is communicated to the clinical team before any procedure.
6. **Reaction response.** A suspected latex reaction is treated as a clinical emergency, managed and documented; the affected individual's record is updated and future latex contact prevented. A staff reaction is also an occupational health matter (ELB-POL-001).
7. **Surveillance and training.** Staff are informed of latex-allergy risk at induction; emerging symptoms (dermatitis, rhinitis, respiratory symptoms on glove use) are reported early so that sensitisation can be managed before it progresses.

## 4. Responsibilities

- **All staff** — use the designated latex-free materials, report glove-related symptoms early, and respect declared patient and colleague allergies.
- **Laboratory Lead** — ensures latex-free gloves and consumables are stocked, that latex is excluded from the culture pathway, and that any retained latex item is risk-assessed.
- **Quality Manager** — maintains the confidential record of declared sensitivities and the latex-content register, and tracks any reaction to closure.
- **Medical Director** — owns this policy, oversees the clinical management of reactions, and approves any local exception in Appendix A.

## 6. Deviations

Introduction of a latex-containing item into the culture pathway, use of powdered gloves near open culture, or a latex contact involving a declared-sensitive individual is recorded and managed under the deviation and CAPA policy (ELB-POL-006) using the deviation log (ELB-LOG-023). A clinical reaction is handled as an adverse event (ELB-POL-005).

## 7. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

The approved latex-free glove and consumable specifications, the list of any retained latex-containing items with their risk assessment, and the flagging convention for declared sensitivities are **locally defined** and recorded in **Appendix A** (flagged 🔧, requiring Medical Director sign-off). The avoidance of NRL exposure and embryotoxic materials is **guideline-anchored** (ISO 15189; WHO occupational health guidance).

## 9. Teaching point

Latex causes two different problems at once: it sensitises the people who wear it, and its proteins and glove powder are toxic to embryos. Both problems are solved by the same simple default — keep latex out. Sensitisation is cumulative and often silent until it is severe, so the early itch under a glove is a signal to act, not to tolerate.
