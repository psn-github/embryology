## 1. Purpose

To define how the Embryology Laboratory records, assesses and resolves deviations from approved procedures and specifications, and how corrective and preventive actions (CAPA) are raised, implemented and verified. The policy turns every departure from "the way we said we would do it" into a documented, closed-out improvement, so that errors are corrected and their causes removed. It is implemented through the deviation-handling procedure (ELB-SOP-003).

## 2. Scope

Applies to all laboratory activities using the couple's own (autologous) gametes and embryos, and to every deviation from an SOP, policy, specification, acceptance criterion or environmental/equipment setpoint. It covers planned deviations (a controlled, pre-approved one-off departure) and unplanned deviations (something that did not go to plan). High-severity events that caused or could cause harm are also handled under the adverse-events policy (ELB-POL-005); the two policies are paired and share the same investigation and CAPA machinery.

## 3. Policy statements

1. **All deviations are recorded.** Any departure from an approved document or specification — equipment out of range, a missed witness step, an out-of-spec consumable, a process not followed — is recorded in the deviation log (ELB-LOG-023), regardless of whether harm resulted.
2. **Containment first.** On identifying a deviation, the immediate concern is to contain it: protect affected material, quarantine affected product, and decide whether work may safely continue.
3. **Assessment and grading.** Each deviation is assessed for impact on patient material, results and safety, and graded. The grade sets the depth of investigation and whether escalation to an adverse event (ELB-POL-005) is required.
4. **Root cause, not symptom.** Recurrent or significant deviations are investigated for root cause (ELB-SOP-003 / ELB-SOP-004). A correction fixes the instance; the preventive action fixes the cause so it does not recur.
5. **CAPA discipline.** Corrective and preventive actions have a named owner, a target date and a defined completion criterion. Actions are tracked to closure and their effectiveness is verified before the deviation is closed.
6. **Planned deviations are pre-approved.** A deliberate, controlled departure (for example a one-off substitution) is risk-assessed and authorised before it is carried out, never retrospectively justified.
7. **Document control feedback.** Where investigation shows an SOP is unclear, wrong or unworkable, the document is revised through change control (ELB-SOP-001) rather than worked around informally.
8. **Trending.** Deviations are trended by type, process and equipment; clusters are escalated to management review to drive systemic improvement.

## 4. Responsibilities

- **All staff** — record deviations promptly and honestly, contain the immediate impact, and complete assigned corrective actions.
- **Laboratory Lead** — assesses and grades deviations, decides whether work continues, and assigns CAPA owners.
- **Quality Manager** — owns the deviation log, coordinates root-cause analysis, verifies CAPA effectiveness and closure, and reports trends to management review.
- **Medical Director** — owns this policy, approves planned deviations of significance, and decides escalation to adverse events.

## 6. Deviations

Failure to record a deviation, working around an SOP without raising one, or leaving a CAPA open beyond its target date without justification is itself a recordable nonconformity in the deviation log (ELB-LOG-023) and is escalated to the Quality Manager. A deviation that caused or could cause patient harm is escalated as an adverse event (ELB-POL-005).

## 7. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

The deviation severity-grading scale, the CAPA target-closure intervals by grade, the effectiveness-verification period, and the threshold at which a deviation cluster triggers management review are **locally defined** and recorded in **Appendix A** (flagged 🔧, requiring Medical Director sign-off). The requirement to control nonconformities and drive corrective and preventive action is **guideline-anchored** (ISO 15189; ESHRE 2015; ASRM 2022).

## 9. Teaching point

A deviation is not a confession, it is data. The laboratory that records the small out-of-range readings and the missed steps is the one that sees the pattern before it becomes a harm. The discipline that separates real CAPA from paperwork is the last step: verifying that the preventive action actually worked, because an action that was implemented but did not change the outcome has not fixed anything — it has only closed a form.
