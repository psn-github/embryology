## 1. Purpose

To define how the Embryology Laboratory receives, assesses and acts on manufacturer recalls, field safety notices and product alerts affecting media, consumables, devices and equipment. The policy ensures that a defective or recalled product is identified, quarantined and removed from use quickly, and that any patient material that may have been affected is traced and reviewed.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all media, oils, consumables, plasticware, cryogenic devices, instruments and equipment used in the laboratory, and to all sources of recall and alert information from manufacturers, suppliers and regulators. It links to inventory and lot control (see register) and equipment maintenance (OMK-SOP-EMB-0060). Only the couple's own (autologous) material is handled, so traceability runs to the affected cycle rather than to any  pathway.

## 3. Policy statements

1. **Active monitoring.** The laboratory maintains channels to receive manufacturer field safety notices, recalls and product alerts, and reviews them promptly. Receipt of an alert is not left to chance; supplier notifications are routed to a named role.
2. **Lot traceability.** Every media bottle, consumable and reagent is recorded with its lot/batch number and expiry against the cycles in which it was used (see register), so that an affected lot can be traced to specific patients and procedures.
3. **Immediate quarantine.** On receipt of a recall or alert, all affected stock is identified by lot number, physically segregated, labelled "QUARANTINE — DO NOT USE", and removed from the active workflow before any further use.
4. **Impact assessment.** A documented assessment determines whether affected product was already used, on which cycles, and whether any gamete or embryo outcome may have been compromised. The assessment is proportionate to the hazard described in the alert.
5. **Patient and clinical notification.** Where used affected product may have caused or contributed to harm, the case is escalated as an adverse event (OMK-POL-EMB-0004) and the treating clinician is informed so that affected patients can be advised.
6. **Disposition and return.** Quarantined product is returned, destroyed or otherwise dispositioned per the manufacturer's instructions and recorded. Replacement stock is verified before use.
7. **Closure and feedback.** Each recall is tracked to closure, with the corrective and preventive actions captured (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005). Recurrent issues with a product or supplier feed back into procurement and supplier evaluation.

## 4. Responsibilities

- **All staff** — do not use any product subject to a current quarantine; report any suspected product defect even before a formal alert is issued.
- **Laboratory Lead / named recall coordinator** — receives alerts, triggers quarantine, runs the impact assessment, and arranges disposition and replacement.
- **Quality Manager** — maintains the recall/alert log, verifies lot traceability, and tracks each event and its CAPA to closure.
- **Medical Director** — owns this policy, decides on patient notification with the treating clinician, and approves the local monitoring sources in Appendix A.

## 6. Deviations

Continued use of a product after a recall, a failure to quarantine, or a break in lot traceability that prevents impact assessment is recorded and managed under the deviation and CAPA policy (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005) using the deviation log (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005). Any case where affected product may have harmed a patient or compromised material is escalated as an adverse event (OMK-POL-EMB-0004).

## 7. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

The list of monitored alert sources, the target time from alert receipt to quarantine, the impact-assessment trigger thresholds, and the named recall-coordinator role are **locally defined** and recorded in **Appendix A** (flagged 🔧, requiring Medical Director sign-off). The requirement for lot traceability and timely action on product safety information is **guideline-anchored** (ISO 15189; ESHRE 2015).

## 9. Teaching point

A recall is only as good as the lot record that lets you answer two questions fast: do we still have any, and did we already use it. The first protects future patients by quarantine; the second protects past patients by traceability. An embryology laboratory cannot recall an embryo, so the value of disciplined lot recording is that it converts a frightening alert into a defined, bounded list of cycles to review.
