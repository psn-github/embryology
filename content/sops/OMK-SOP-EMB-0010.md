#### Purpose

To describe the procedure for analysing semen parameters and post vasectomy analysis to required standards as set out in WHO (2021), ABA Guidelines for good Practice (2012) and Guidelines for Post Vasectomy analysis (2016). Semen samples can be analysed for patients prior to coming through for fertility treatment to determine the likely treatment type, for prospective sperm  to determine their suitability or for patients undergoing fertility assessment via a GP or privately. Post vasectomy analysis is carried out for patients following vasectomy to assess the sample for the presence of sperm.

Applicable to all embryologists, andrologists and practitioners deemed competent to perform semen analysis:

##### 5.2 Equipment Checks

Prior to performing the semen analysis procedure, the equipment to be used must be checked to ensure it is operating optimally.

The flowhood (if applicable) must be checked to ensure the flow is on and the hood is clear of used pots/pipettes and tubes.

Ensure the microscope heated stage (if applicable) is switched on and set at 37°C at least 10 minutes prior to examination of samples.

##### 5.3 Witnessing Checks (Full Name, Clinic Number & D.O.B.)

There should be a minimum of 3 patient identifiers on the sample pot e.g. full name, D.O.B. & clinic number.

Ask the patient to state their full name and D.O.B. Check this information along with their clinic or hospital number matches the patient’s laboratory record and sample pot. Record the number of day’s abstinence and any illness/medication in the last 2-3 months. The above checks must be performed whether the sample is produced on-site or off-site.

For on-site production, hand the patient the sample pot and provide the patient with sample production instructions. Instruct the patient to inform the laboratory team if not all the sample is collected/or record this on the sample production form.

###### 5.3.1 Post Vasectomy Analysis Rejection Criteria

Samples that are incorrectly labelled, unlabelled, where the witnessing step has not been fully completed or whereby there is doubt as to its identification must be rejected.

For post vasectomy samples it is imperative that the patient confirms whether the whole sample has been collected or not. This can be recorded on the sample production form or be confirmed verbally post-production as part of the sample hand-over procedure. This must be clearly documented in the laboratory notes. **Incomplete samples for post vasectomy analysis must be rejected and a further appointment made for the patient.**

See section 5.12 for the strict criteria that must be met for clearance to be given.

###### 5.3.2 Diagnostic Semen Analysis Rejection Criteria

Samples that are incorrectly labelled, unlabelled, where the witnessing step has not been fully completed or whereby there is doubt as to its identification, must be rejected.

Samples for diagnostic semen analysis may still be analysed in the following scenarios, however, a note should be made on the report and the patient must be made aware that they may need a repeat analysis if there are any abnormal parameters.

-   Patient has not adhered to correct abstinence period
-   Patient has had any illness/medication known to affect sperm quality
-   Patient has not collected a complete sample (except for Post vasectomy analysis, which must be rejected)
-   Patient has not delivered the sample to the unit within 50 minutes of production or has not maintained the correct temperature of the sample during transport to the lab (if producing off site).

Once the sample has been produced, transfer the sample to the flowhood/laboratory bench or warming oven if available.

Only one sample may be processed in a flowhood at any one time.

##### 5.4 Sample Production

Semen analysis should be performed following 2-7 days abstinence.

Semen samples should where possible be produced on site in order to limit temperature variations and control time between collection and analysis.

Semen samples should be collected via masturbation into pre-weighed, labelled, plastic containers which are clean, wide-mouthed and that have ideally been confirmed to be non-toxic to sperm.

If the patient is to produce on site, the sample production room must be cleaned prior to use by wiping all surfaces (including door handles, the sample production hatch, chairs, pens etc.) in the production room using alcohol free disinfectant wipes or Oosafe.

If the patient is unable to produce on site, offer the patient the option to produce off site. Provide the patient with a pre-weighed and pre-labelled sample pot. If the sample is produced off-site the patient must sign an off-site production declaration stating that the sample is his own and has not been tampered with. Advise the patient to keep the sample around body temperature and to bring it to the clinic within 50 minutes of production.

Patients may also be given the option of producing the sample into a non-toxic condom.

##### 5.5 Preparation for the Procedure

Turn on the heated stage and set to 37°C for at least 10 minutes prior to use.

Remove reagents from the fridge and allow them to warm to room temperature prior to use.

Pre-weigh the labelled sample pot prior to the patient producing the sample, note the weight of the empty pot on the lid.

##### 5.6 Timing of Semen Analysis

Begin initial analysis of the semen sample 15-60 minutes post-production and following sample liquefication.

If after 30 minutes the sample fails to liquefy, place the sample on to a heated stage/warming oven set at 37°C, and re-assess after 30 minutes (with repeated pipetting). If after 60 minutes the sample is still not liquefied, an equal volume of pre-equilibrated media may be added, however this must be reported and accounted for when calculating sperm concentration.

Delay or failure of the sample to liquefy after 30 or 60 minutes should be noted on the final report.

##### 5.7 Semen Analysis

This section is the main text of the SOP. It details the procedure for the task to be performed.

Assess the volume, viscosity, concentration, motility, morphology, pH and presence of anti-sperm antibodies of the initial semen sample.

Assessments of round cells and viability are performed depending on the patient’s clinical history and/or sample characteristics.

Gloves must be worn when handling human semen.

###### 5.7.1 Assessment of Sample Volume (Reference value ≥1.4ml)

The sample pot must be pre-weighed and the weight recorded. Once the patient has produced the sample, weigh the pot containing the semen sample and note the weight.

To calculate the sample volume, subtract the weight of the empty sample pot from the weight of the pot + sample. Calculate the sample volume assuming the density of semen to be 1g/ml.

###### 5.7.2 Initial Macroscopic Assessment of Viscosity and Appearance

Prior to analysis the sample should be mixed by either manually swirling for 15-30 seconds or by using a serological/Pasteur pipette and thoroughly mixing the sample by pipetting up and down several times.

Assess the viscosity by allowing semen to drop by gravity from a serological pipette and observing the length of the thread. A thread of >2 cm indicates abnormal viscosity. Record the sample viscosity as ‘viscous’ or ‘non-viscous’.

Any abnormal appearance should be recorded, for example a pinkish colour may indicate blood is present.

For samples that are extremely viscous and where assessment may be difficult, the sample should be treated as above for delayed liquefaction prior to analysis.

###### 5.7.3 Assessment of Motility (Reference value A + B= ≥30%)

Perform motility assessment within 60 minutes of sample production, and ideally within 30 minutes following liquefaction. Perform the motility assessment on a heated stage at 37°C where possible.

Mix the sample and then place 10 µl of semen onto a clear glass slide and cover with a glass cover slip.

Re-mix the sample and place a replicate 10µl drop of semen on to a glass slide and cover with a glass coverslip.

Observe the slides at X200-400 magnification. Avoiding areas that are close to the edge of the coverslip, grade the motility of 200 sperm per replicate in a minimum of 5 randomly chosen fields using the following criteria:

**Grade A –** rapid progressive motility (≥25µm/s)

**Grade B –** slow or sluggish motility (5 - <25 µm/s)

**Grade C –** non-progressive motility (<5 µm/s)

**Grade D –** immotile (no active tail movement)

Note: If 200 sperm have been counted in one replicate before all grades have been scored, continue counting until all grades have been scored on that field.

Calculate the percentages of each grade, and then calculate the percentage of total progressive motile.

Assess if there is an acceptable difference between the 2 replicate values for the most frequent grade of motility noted (motile or immotile), by referring to the table below (based on 95% confidence interval).

Worked example:

In replicate one: 200 sperm are counted to give the following motility grades (%):

Motility = 10/26/4/60 and a total progressive motility of 10 + 26 = 36%

In replicate two: 210 sperm are counted and converted to percentages to give:

Motility = 8/26/3/63 and a total progressive motility of 8 + 26 = 34%

The most frequent grade for both replicates above (motile or immotile) is the immotile grade D (60 & 63) therefore the average and difference between the two replicates is calculated for this grade (and rounded up to a whole number):

Average = 60 + 63 /2 = 61.5 = 62

Difference = 63-60 = 3

By referring to table 1 this difference (3) is seen to be less than that found by chance alone for the calculated average of 62 (acceptable difference = 9), so the values are accepted.

Average = a’s = (10+8)/2, b’s = (26 + 26)/2, c’s = (4+3)/2 and d’s = (60+63)/2

Average motility = 9/26/3.5/61.5

Therefore reported motility = 9/26/3/62

###### 5.7.4 Assessment of Concentration (Reference value ≥16M/ml)

Assess the appropriate dilution to be used according to the number of sperm per HPF observed on a wet preparation at x400 in a minimum of 3 fields. A minimum volume of 50µl semen should be used to make up dilutions, and the total volume should be a minimum of 200 µl.

Dilutions of the semen sample should be made immediately after mixing and within 60 minutes of sample production. Aliquots of water should be prepared the same day as used to prevent evaporation.

Attach the cover slip to the haemocytometer until Newtons Rings are visible. The cover slip must be a thickness of 0.44mm #4.

Prepare the first dilution using the dilutions described above, by pipetting the required volume of semen into in an Eppendorf tube containing the required volume of water/fixative. Ensure the pipette tip is rinsed via aspirating up and down a few times.

Vortex the diluted sample for 15 seconds at high speed to mix thoroughly, and immediately remove an aliquot of 8-10 µl. Gently pipette approximately 8-10µl of the first diluted sample onto one side of the haemocytometer chamber, allowing the chamber to fill by capillary action.

Prepare a second replicate dilution as above and immediately pipette approximately 8-10µl of the second dilution into the other side of the haemocytometer chamber.

Allow the chamber to settle for 4 minutes in a humid chamber before analysis. Observe the chamber at X200-400 magnification.

In the central grid each row consists of 5 large squares.

A row of 5 squares in the central grid

**_Image 2:_** _The Central Grid (5 in the image above)_

A minimum of 200 sperm must be counted. Count only morphologically mature cells with tails.

When assessing the concentration 5,10, or 25 squares must always be counted even if a count of 200 sperm has already been reached, i.e. if 4 squares have been counted and 200 sperm have been reached, the 5th square must still be counted.

If a count of 200 sperm is not reached in the central grid, then the 2 grids adjacent should be counted (grids 4 and 6 in image 3 below):

![](assets/figures/OMK-SOP-EMB-0010/fig-4.png)

**_Image 3_** _– Grids 4 and 6_

The 2 grids (4 and 6) are again made up of 5 rows, however each row consists of 4 large squares.

If 200 sperm cannot be counted in grids 5,6 and 4 then a higher dilution factor should be used if possible. For very low concentrations using a 1:2 dilution all 9 grids may be assessed with the same principles as above. If 200 sperm cannot be counted using a 1:2 dilution in all 9 grids, a note should be made on the report that <200 sperm counted.

Counting procedure

Observe the central grid on one side of the haemocytometer.

Count a minimum of 5 squares in the central grid. If 200 sperm are counted record the number of sperm counted in the 5 squares.

If a minimum of 200 sperm are not observed in the first 5 squares, then continue counting to 10 squares in the central grid, and record the results.

If 200 sperm are not observed in the 10 squares then continue counting all 25 squares of the central grid and record the results.

If the whole of the central grid (25 squares) has been counted and 200 sperm have not been observed, either prepare new dilutions using the next dilution factor, if possible, or count the whole of one of the grids adjacent to the central grid (grids 4 or 6). Continue to count whole grids (grids 4,5,6,7,8,9) until a minimum of 200 sperm has been counted.

Once a minimum of 200 sperm have been counted make a note of the number of either squares or whole grids assessed to count 200 sperm and count the same number of squares/grids on the second side of the haemocytometer chamber.

Calculate the sum and difference of the two results. Determine the acceptability of the difference from the table below.

###### 5.7.5 Calculation of Total Sperm Number (Reference value Total Sperm Number ≥39M)

The total sperm number should be reported for each sample. This is calculated by multiplying the concentration of sperm in millions per ml by the volume of the whole sample.

###### 5.7.6 Assessment of Morphology (Reference value normal forms ≥4%)

Place 5µl of semen onto a pre-stained morphology slide and cover with a cover slip. Allow the slide to air dry for 15-30 minutes. Observe the slide at X1000 magnification under oil immersion. Grade 200 whole sperm as ‘normal’ or ‘abnormal’ according to WHO 2021 criteria. Note the result as a percentage of ‘normal’ forms.

The categories of the abnormalities (i.e. head, neck and mid-piece, tail and cytoplasmic residues) do not need to be specified in the report however operators should be able to categorise these (i.e. via participation in IQA and EQA).

For samples with low concentrations (<2M/ml) report that there are too few to accurately assess the morphology. Alternatively, the sample may be concentrated down by centrifugation prior to assessment. However, this should be reported in the report as centrifugation may affect the morphology result.

For sperm to be considered ‘normal’, both the tail and head must be normal. All borderline sperm must be considered ‘abnormal’.

Normal sperm have smooth, oval shaped head with a well-defined acrosomal area comprising 40-70% of the head area. The acrosomal area should not contain any large vacuoles and not more than two small vacuoles. There should be no vacuoles in the post acrosomal region.

The midpiece should be slender and regular and approximately the same length as the head. The axis of the midpiece should be aligned with the axis of the sperm head.

Cytoplasmic droplets should be < 1/3 of the size of the sperm head.

The tail should be regular and thinner than the midpiece and around ten times the length of the head (approximately 45µm long).

If prevalence of specific defects are noted in the assessment, such as sperm with heads lacking acrosomes (‘globozoospermia’) or ‘pinheads’ (free tails with no head structure), these should be reported.

###### 5.7.7 Assessment of Anti-Sperm Antibodies IgG (Reference value <50%)

Allow the reagents to warm to room temperature.

Thoroughly mix the reagent(s). Add 5µl of each IgG reagent (latex beads and anti-serum to IgG) to a clear glass slide along with 5µl of semen. Mix on the slide using the pipette tip. Place a cover slip on top.

Allow the sample to settle for 3 minutes then observe the slide under x200-400 magnification.

Assess 200 motile sperm for the presence of beads bound to them. Convert the number of sperm showing evidence of binding to a percentage.

If initial assessment shows no binding, assess again after 10 minutes to confirm the result.

###### 5.7.8 Assessment of pH (Reference Range pH ≥ 7.2)

Perform the pH assessment within 1 hour of sample production.

Place a drop of the semen sample on the pH paper. Wait for the colour of the impregnated zone to become uniform. Compare the colour to the calibration strip and read the pH.

###### 5.7.9. Assessment of Viability, if motility <5%, (Reference value ≥ 54% live)

Perform the Vitality assessment within 1 hour of production, ideally at 30 minutes following liquefaction using Vitalscreen (Eosin and Nigrosin exclusion).

Mix 50µl of semen with 2 drops of reagent 1 in an Eppendorf tube, mixing gently, and after 30 seconds add 3 drops of reagent 2. Mix thoroughly. Within 30 seconds place a 20µl drop of mixture on to a clear glass slide and make a smear using the edge of a cover slip. Cover with a cover slip and assess immediately at X400. Alternatively assess the slide at X1000 magnification under oil immersion. Assess 200 sperm as unstained (live) or stained (dead), calculate the percentage viable (live).

###### 5.7.10 Assessment of Round Cells, if indicated (Reference value <1M/ml Peroxidase Positive Cells)

An assessment of round cells in the sample can be carried out in the same way as concentration as decsribed above using the haemocytometer chamber, using an appropriate dilution. If the round cell count is found to exceed 5M/ml, an assessment using LeucoScreen Plus should be performed to determine the concentration of peroxidase positive cells. Record the concentration of total positive round cells.

To perform a LeucoScreen assessment:

Prepare a working solution by adding 200µl Reagent 1, 5µl of Reagent 2 and 1ml of Reagent 3 to an Eppendorf tube and mixing thoroughly.

This working solution can be kept for 48 hours if protected from sunlight and stored at 2-8°C.

Mix 10 µl of sperm with 20 µl of working solution in an Eppendorf and mix thoroughly. Incubate for 2 minutes at room temperature in the dark.

Transfer 10µl of the mix to a glass slide and add 10µl of Reagent 4. Mix thoroughly using the pipette tip/edge of the coverslip.

Cover with a coverslip and assess the slide.

Observing at least 20 fields at X400 magnification, count and classify (peroxidase positive or peroxidase negative) a total of 200 round cells.

###### 5.7.11 Additional Microscopic Assessments (if indicated)

Agglutination (where motile sperm stick together i.e. head-to-head, tail-to-tail, tail tip-to-tail tip, or mixed) can be assessed and recorded if noticed to be high and a comment made on the report as agglutination ‘+ (<10 sperm per agglutinate), ++ (10-50 sperm per agglutinate), or +++ (agglutinates of >50 sperm). A note should be made on the report of the type of attachment.

If sperm aggregates (where immotile sperm stick together, stick to motile sperm or stick to mucus strands or debris) are noticed in the sample a note of this can be made on the report.

If debris or bacteria is noticed in the sperm sample, this can also be noted on the report as debris or bacteria ‘+, ++ or +++’.

###### 5.7.12 Post Examination Procedure

Following completion of the semen analysis, clear the flowhood of any used consumables. Use Oosafe ± sterile water to clean the flowhood/bench.

Dispose of any used slides/pipettes/pots/tubes in clinical waste (sharps bins for sharp objects).

Proceed to writing the semen analysis report.

##### 5.8 Low Sperm Numbers & Suspected Azoospermia

If too few sperm are seen on the haemocytometer chamber in 9 grids for an accurate assessment (<25 sperm in each whole chamber using a 1:2 dilution i.e. <55,555 sperm/ml), the number of sperm observed should be reported, however a note needs to be made on the report that too few sperm are seen for accurate assessment (<56,000).

Assess the number of sperm seen in the initial 10µl per HPF for the two replicates and whether sperm seen are motile/twitching/immotile.

If only immotile or twitching sperm are seen in the initial 10µl, samples may be concentrated to a smaller volume by centrifugation at 0.3-0.8rcf and then re-assessed for the presence of motile sperm.

If no sperm are seen in the initial 10µl slide, the sample should be assessed for the presence of sperm using either centrifugation method (2002 BAS guidelines) or fixed depth Post Vasectomy slides (2016 Guidelines). These processes are outlined in 5.11 below.

If no sperm are seen in the sample then a follow up appointment must be made in 2-3 months’ time in order to diagnose azoospermia.

At the end of the semen analysis, clear the flowhood/bench of any used consumables. Use Oosafe ± sterile water to clean the flowhood/bench.

##### 5.9 Assessment of Motility and Concentration using CASA systems

CASA systems (Computer Aided Semen analysis) systems can be used as an alternative to manual methods to assess concentration and motility however they must be validated against a haemocytometer chamber.

###### 5.9.1 SCA CASA (Microptics)

To be suitable for assessment via the CASA, samples should have a minimum of >15 sperm per X400 HPF.

If samples exceed >50M/ml they should be diluted 1:1, 1:2 or 1:3 with media prior to assessment, by mixing 50µl of semen with: 50µl, 100µl or 150µl of media (as required) in an Eppendorf tube. The dilution factor must be taken into account when assessing on the CASA, by selecting a dilution factor (as described below). A note should be made on the report of the dilution factor used.

Use a fixed depth (20µm) chamber suitable for use with the CASA system (such as a Leja 20 slide) and pipette 8-10 µl of semen into the chamber(s) of the slide.

Observe the filling time of the chamber and adjust the viscosity settings as required in the CASA settings.

<2-3s – low viscosity

4-10s – medium viscosity

\>10s – high viscosity

Allow the slide to settle for around 2 minutes, and then place the slide onto the microscope. For analysis on the CASA, the slide should be observed under x100 magnification.

**5.10 Retrograde Analysis**

Retrograde samples must be produced on site so that the sample can be prepared as quickly as possible.

The patient is required to adjust the osmolality and pH of their urine prior to sperm production.

Advise the patient to take 2 Alka-Seltzer tablets or a 20ml Potassium Citrate mixture the night before producing the sample and again 2-4 hours prior to sample production.

On the morning of sample production ask the patient to avoid caffeine/fruit juice and drink approximately 500ml of water.

Immediately prior to sample production the patient must empty their bladder. Sample production must follow immediately afterwards.

Advise the patient to collect any ejaculate produced into the semen pot and then empty their bladder and collect the urine into the sterile jug provided.

Both semen and urine samples must be passed to the laboratory for immediate analysis.

If an adequate number of motile sperm for analysis are in the semen sample, proceed with analysing the semen sample alone.

If no sperm or an inadequate number are found in the semen sample proceed with processing the urine sample. Determine the pH of the urine sample.

Transfer the urine to pre-labelled conical tubes and centrifuge at 0.8 r.c.f. for 5 minutes.

Remove the pellet and re-suspend in 0.5ml of pre-equilibrated media. Assess the suspension for the presence of sperm. Report if any sperm are seen in the urine. Concentration, motility and morphology assessments as described above should be performed.

Clear the flowhood/bench of any used consumables. Use Oosafe ± sterile water to clean the flowhood/bench.

**5.11 Reporting & Interpreting the Results**

Report the results of each test performed in the patient management system (a paper copy of the report may also be produced).

Interpretation of semen analysis results should only be done by members of staff deemed competent to do so.

Interpretation should consider guidance as set out in WHO 2021. The lower fifth percentile of men (the level under which only semen parameters from 5% of the men in the reference population were found to achieve a pregnancy within 1 year of trying (TTC <1 year) as per the WHO 2021) may be used as a guide when interpreting results, however it shouldn’t be used as a limit between fertile and infertile men.

Interpretation should also take in to account the patient’s clinical history and previous results. A single semen sample may not be representative and therefore it may be beneficial to request a further analysis.

For patients undergoing fertility treatment, treatment recommendations based on the sample should take into account reference values for the lower fifth percentile values as per WHO (2021) and recommendations made by referring to TFP1-POL-EMB-0001.


**5.12 Post Vasectomy Analysis**

Patients who have had a vasectomy will be given a post vasectomy semen assessment appointment to assess the presence of sperm in the sample. Clearance can be provided following the analysis of a single sample providing the strict criteria described below have been met. If the strict criteria below have not been met, a second sample must be requested.

Patients are advised to have their initial post vasectomy analysis a minimum of 12 weeks post vasectomy and after a minimum of 20 ejaculates.

The abstinence period should be 2-7 days for post vasectomy assessment for clearance to be given.

Incomplete samples for Post Vasectomy analysis must be rejected and a further appointment made for the patient.

Post vasectomy samples must be examined for the presence of sperm within 4 hours of production, however, it is recommended that samples are examined within 1 hour, because if non-motile sperm are observed then a further sample must then be analysed within 1 hour of production.

Provided the sample has been assessed within the recommended time period, and that sample collection criteria and lab procedures have been met and are strictly followed, one clear sample is sufficient for clearance.

However, a second sample to determine clearance can be carried out on request from the referrer or as per local policy.

Patients are advised to have any repeat appointments a minimum of 4-6 weeks after their initial assessment.

Patients that have continued immotile sperm seen in the sample after 7 months post-surgery may be given special clearance at the discretion of the clinic/referrer.

Special clearance must only be given if <100,000/ml non motile sperm are observed and after assessment of a minimum of 2 samples. It must not be given if any motile sperm are observed.

###### 5.12.1 Initial Assessment

Record the volume and pH of the sample as described above.

With a serological/Pasteur pipette thoroughly mix the sample and place 10µl of semen onto a clear glass slide and cover with a glass cover slip. Observe the slide at X200-400 magnification for the presence of sperm.

Record if any sperm are seen in the wet preparation, then report of the approximate number of sperm seen on the wet preparation per HPF and if the sperm seen were motile, twitching or immotile.

If numbers of sperm seen exceed >1M/ml carry out a full semen analysis.

If no sperm is seen in the initial 10µl, the sample must be assessed by either method below:

###### 5.12.2 Option 1: Assessment on Deep Chamber 100um PVSA slide (2016 guidelines)

Transfer 25µl of sample into a fixed-depth 100µm post vasectomy slide. The filled slide should then be stored in a humid atmosphere for a minimum of 15 minutes prior to examination.

Examine the slide for the presence of sperm on a phase contrast microscope under x200 magnification. The entire slide must be examined for the presence of sperm.

If sperm are seen, make a note of the number of sperm seen in the PVSA slide on the report and report whether the sperm seen were motile.

If no sperm are seen, report that no sperm were seen in the fixed 100µm depth post vasectomy slides.

###### 5.12.3 Option 2: Centrifuge Sample (2002 BAS Guidelines)

Transfer 1ml of sample into a tube/Eppendorf and centrifuge at 3.0 r.c.f. for 15 minutes.

Remove the supernatant and resuspend the pellet in a volume of 50µl.

Place two 10µl drops into a lake dish, overlay with oil and assess the drops on an inverted microscope for the presence of sperm.

If sperm is seen, make a note of this on the report and report whether the sperm seen were motile.

If no sperm are seen, report that no sperm were seen in the pellet post centrifugation.

Clear the flowhood of any used consumables. Use Oosafe ± sterile water to clean the flowhood.
