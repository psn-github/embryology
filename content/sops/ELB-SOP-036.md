## 1. Purpose

To define how cryogenic storage tanks (dewars) holding the laboratory's vitrified embryos, oocytes and sperm are specified, identified, organised, secured and maintained, so that stored material is held continuously at cryogenic temperature with full traceability and under controlled access.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all fixed and transport liquid-nitrogen (LN₂) dewars used to store autologous gametes and embryos in the Embryology Laboratory. The day-to-day operations of filling, level/temperature monitoring, use and transport are defined in ELB-SOP-037; this SOP covers the tanks themselves — their identity, inventory structure, security and condition. Donor and surrogacy material is out of scope.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Laboratory Lead** — owns the tank register, assigns each tank a role, and authorises decommissioning or replacement.
- **Designated cryostore custodian** — maintains the inventory (ELB-LOG-016), controls access keys/codes (ELB-POL-007) and reports tank condition.
- **Embryologist** — places and retrieves material only with witnessing (ELB-SOP-002) and records every movement in the inventory.
- **Quality Manager** — audits inventory accuracy, access logs and tank maintenance.

## 4. Definitions & abbreviations

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Dewar / tank | Vacuum-insulated vessel holding LN₂ for cryogenic storage |
| Vapour-phase tank | Tank storing material in cold nitrogen vapour above a liquid reservoir |
| Liquid-phase tank | Tank storing material immersed in LN₂ |
| Canister / canister position | The numbered subdivision of a tank that holds canes/goblets |
| Quarantine tank | A tank or position holding material pending an identity, consent or screening resolution |

## 5. Equipment, materials & media

- Vacuum-insulated LN₂ dewars of the type and hold-time specified for the suite, each uniquely labelled with a tank ID.
- Canisters, canes, goblets and visotubes mapped to numbered positions.
- Tank-mounted or external level/temperature sensors with local and remote alarm output (operation in ELB-SOP-037).
- A roller base or trolley for any movable tank; spill containment and ventilation appropriate to the room.
- GHS-labelled signage at the cryostore and the SDS for nitrogen on file (ELB-POL-002).

> **HAZARD — asphyxiation and cold-burn.** Nitrogen is an inert, colourless, odourless simple asphyxiant; an LN₂ release expands roughly 700-fold to gas and can displace breathable air without warning. Contact with LN₂ or cold surfaces causes cryogenic burns. The cryostore is fitted with oxygen-depletion monitoring; cryo PPE and the safety controls in ELB-SOP-037 apply whenever a tank is opened or handled.

## 6. Pre-procedure checks

1. Confirm each tank carries a legible, unique tank ID and a current external level/condition status.
2. Confirm the room oxygen monitor is powered, in date for calibration and not in alarm before working at a tank (ELB-SOP-037).
3. Confirm the tank's inventory map (ELB-LOG-016) is reconciled and that the intended canister/cane position is correct before placement or retrieval.
4. Confirm the tank shows no excessive external frosting, sweating or vacuum loss; report any such sign before use.

> **CRITICAL — security and access.** Tanks are kept in the access-controlled cryostore. Only authorised staff open a tank, and every entry and movement is recorded under the cryostorage security and access policy (ELB-POL-007).

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Tank identification and inventory structure
1. Assign each tank a unique ID and a defined role (general storage, quarantine, transport).
2. Map every canister position and cane/goblet to a position reference in the inventory (ELB-LOG-016).
3. Store only material whose identity and location are recorded; never place an unlabelled or unwitnessed item.

### 7.2 Placement and retrieval
1. Verify patient identity and the target position with a witness (Section 8) before opening the tank.
2. Work efficiently to limit warming of neighbouring positions; keep canes below the frost line during handling.
3. Update the inventory immediately on placement or retrieval — position occupied or emptied — and re-witness the entry.

### 7.3 Tank condition and maintenance
1. Monitor each tank's hold performance against expected static evaporation; investigate any tank whose LN₂ consumption rises.
2. Record routine condition checks; segregate and transfer contents from any tank showing vacuum failure or abnormal loss to a healthy tank under witnessing, then quarantine the suspect tank from service.
3. Decommission or service a tank only with Laboratory Lead authorisation and after its contents are confirmed relocated in the inventory.

## 8. Critical control points & witnessing

Witnessing is mandatory at every placement, retrieval and inter-tank transfer (ELB-SOP-002). RI Witness performs the electronic match; a manual second-person check is the fallback if EW is unavailable.

| Checkpoint | What is verified | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **W1** | Patient identity and target tank/canister/cane before opening | EW match + inventory cross-check |
| **W2** | Inventory entry (occupied/emptied/moved) matches the physical action | EW / second-person |

> **CRITICAL CONTROL POINT.** Material is never placed in or moved between tanks without a confirmed witness match and an inventory update. A position recorded but not physically occupied — or occupied but not recorded — is a traceability failure and is escalated immediately.

## 9. Acceptance / rejection criteria

- A tank is **fit for storage** when it holds LN₂ above the minimum working level 🔧, shows stable temperature within alarm thresholds 🔧 (Appendix A), and has no sign of vacuum loss.
- A tank is **withdrawn from service** if it breaches level/temperature thresholds, shows rising evaporation, or shows external frosting/sweating consistent with vacuum failure; its contents are transferred under witnessing.

## 10. Deviations

Tank vacuum failure, an out-of-threshold level or temperature, a missed alarm, or any inventory mismatch is managed under the deviation and CAPA policy (ELB-POL-006) and recorded in the deviation log (ELB-LOG-023). Any event threatening stored material is also a reportable adverse event (ELB-POL-005). Filling and monitoring detail is in ELB-SOP-037.

## 11. Records generated

- Cryostorage inventory (tank/canister/cane/position) — **ELB-LOG-016**.
- LN₂ fill / tank monitoring log — **ELB-LOG-021** (operated under ELB-SOP-037).
- Any deviation entry — **ELB-LOG-023**.

## 12. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

Storage in LN₂ phase appropriate to the tank type is **guideline-anchored** (ESHRE 2015). Values tabulated in **Appendix A (ELB-APP-A)** include: the minimum working LN₂ level per tank 🔧, the tank temperature and level alarm thresholds 🔧, the static-evaporation tolerance used to flag vacuum loss 🔧, and the check frequency 🔧. Each locally defined value is flagged 🔧 in Appendix A and requires Medical Director sign-off. Nitrogen hazard classification follows **GHS** (asphyxiant; SDS on file).

## 14. Teaching point

A storage tank is the one piece of equipment in the laboratory whose failure is irreversible and silent. It does not beep when a cane is mis-placed and it does not announce a slow vacuum leak — it simply costs the couple their embryos days or weeks later. The defences are mundane and absolute: a unique ID on every tank, a witnessed and accurate inventory for every position, and a healthy distrust of any tank that frosts, sweats or drinks more nitrogen than it used to.
