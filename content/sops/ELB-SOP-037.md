## 1. Purpose

To define the safe handling, dispensing, transport and continuous monitoring of liquid nitrogen (LN₂) and the cryogenic storage tanks it serves, so that stored gametes and embryos remain at cryogenic temperature, LN₂ is used without harm to staff, and any threat to storage is detected and acted on promptly.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all routine use of LN₂ in the Embryology Laboratory: filling and topping-up storage and transport dewars, decanting LN₂ for procedures, transporting material between locations, and the ongoing monitoring of tank level, temperature and alarms. The tanks themselves — their identity, inventory and condition — are specified in ELB-SOP-036. Temperature monitoring across the wider suite is in ELB-SOP-038. Donor and surrogacy material is out of scope.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Designated cryostore custodian** — performs and records fills, monitors levels/temperature and alarms, and orders LN₂ supply (ELB-LOG-021).
- **All staff handling LN₂** — wear cryo PPE, follow the safety controls, and never work alone at a tank where required.
- **Laboratory Lead** — sets the monitoring schedule and alarm response, and authorises transport of stored material.
- **Quality Manager** — audits the fill/monitoring log and alarm-response records.

## 4. Definitions & abbreviations

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| LN₂ | Liquid nitrogen (boiling point ≈ −196 °C) |
| Simple asphyxiant | A gas that displaces oxygen without toxic effect, causing hypoxia |
| Oxygen-depletion monitor | Fixed/personal alarm that warns when room O₂ falls below a safe threshold |
| Static evaporation | The normal LN₂ loss of a sealed tank, used as a health indicator |
| Dry shipper | A transport dewar holding LN₂ in an absorbent matrix, dispensing only vapour |

## 5. Equipment, materials & media

- Bulk LN₂ supply or supply dewar; transfer hose/phase separator and a filling funnel as required.
- Storage dewars and transport dewars / dry shippers (ELB-SOP-036).
- Tank level/temperature sensors with local audible alarm and remote/out-of-hours alerting.
- Fixed room oxygen-depletion monitor and, where required, a personal O₂ alarm.
- Cryo PPE: full face shield, insulated cryo gloves (loose-cuffed), long apron, long sleeves and closed footwear (ELB-POL-001, ELB-POL-002).
- GHS signage and the nitrogen SDS on file.

> **HAZARD — asphyxiation.** LN₂ expands roughly 700-fold to gaseous nitrogen, which is colourless, odourless and displaces breathable air. Oxygen depletion gives no warning to the senses. Never enter a cryostore that is in O₂ alarm; ventilate and allow the alarm to clear before re-entry. Treat every spill in an enclosed space as an oxygen-depletion event.

> **HAZARD — cold-burn and pressure.** LN₂ and frosted surfaces cause severe cryogenic burns; trapped LN₂ can build pressure and eject liquid. Wear a face shield and cryo gloves, pour slowly, keep skin and clothing clear, and never seal LN₂ in an unvented container.

## 6. Pre-procedure checks

1. Confirm the room oxygen-depletion monitor is powered, in calibration date and not in alarm before entering or working.
2. Confirm cryo PPE is worn and intact, and that a second person is present where the schedule requires it.
3. Confirm the LN₂ supply is adequate and the transfer equipment is undamaged and dry.
4. Confirm the target tank ID, its current level/temperature reading and that it is not in alarm (ELB-SOP-036).

> **CRITICAL — security and access.** LN₂ work and any access to storage tanks is confined to authorised staff in the access-controlled cryostore, per the cryostorage security and access policy (ELB-POL-007). Tanks are never left unsecured or accessible to unauthorised persons.

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Filling / topping-up
1. Verify O₂ monitor status and don cryo PPE.
2. Connect the transfer line / position the funnel; open the supply slowly and fill to the tank's maximum working level, watching for splash-back.
3. Stop at the defined fill level; close the supply, vent residual pressure and disconnect.
4. Record the date, time, tank ID, pre- and post-fill level, volume added and operator on the LN₂ fill / monitoring log (ELB-LOG-021).

### 7.2 Decanting for procedures
1. Decant only the LN₂ needed into a vented working dewar; never overfill or carry an open dewar through occupied corridors without need.
2. Keep the working dewar within the controlled area and return unused LN₂ safely.

### 7.3 Routine monitoring
1. At the defined frequency 🔧 (Appendix A), record each tank's LN₂ level and temperature against its alarm thresholds 🔧 (ELB-LOG-021).
2. Confirm the audible and remote/out-of-hours alarms are armed and the contact list is current.
3. Investigate any rising static evaporation or downward level trend before it reaches the alarm threshold.

### 7.4 Alarm response
1. On a level/temperature/O₂ alarm, follow the documented response: protect staff first (ventilate, evacuate on O₂ alarm), then protect storage.
2. For a tank alarm, top up or transfer contents to a healthy tank under witnessing (ELB-SOP-036, ELB-SOP-002); for an O₂ alarm do not enter until cleared.
3. Record the alarm, cause and action as a deviation (ELB-LOG-023).

### 7.5 Transport
1. Transport stored material only in a dry shipper or transport dewar conditioned per its IFU, secured upright and labelled, with consent and inventory updated.
2. Maintain identity and chain of custody throughout (ELB-SOP-002); verify temperature integrity on arrival and record it.

## 8. Critical control points & witnessing

Witnessing applies to any movement of stored material during transfer or transport (ELB-SOP-002). Fill and monitoring entries are recorded by the custodian and audited.

| Checkpoint | What is verified | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **W1** | Patient identity and tank position before any transfer of material | EW match + inventory cross-check |
| **W2** | Material loaded for transport matches the inventory and consent | EW / second-person + chain of custody |

> **CRITICAL CONTROL POINT.** Staff safety takes precedence over storage in every alarm. On an oxygen alarm no one enters the cryostore, regardless of what is stored there — a tank can be refilled, an asphyxiation cannot be undone.

## 9. Acceptance / rejection criteria

- A fill is **acceptable** when the post-fill level is at the defined maximum working level and no alarm condition remains.
- A tank **passes monitoring** when level and temperature are within the alarm thresholds 🔧 and static evaporation is within tolerance 🔧 (Appendix A); otherwise it is escalated and, if failing, withdrawn (ELB-SOP-036).
- A transport is **acceptable** when arrival temperature integrity is confirmed and chain of custody is unbroken.

## 10. Deviations

A low-level/high-temperature alarm, an O₂ alarm, a spill, a PPE failure, rising evaporation, or any transport temperature excursion is managed under the deviation and CAPA policy (ELB-POL-006) and recorded in the deviation log (ELB-LOG-023). Events threatening stored material or staff safety are reportable adverse events (ELB-POL-005).

## 11. Records generated

- LN₂ fill / tank monitoring log (levels, temperatures, alarms, fills) — **ELB-LOG-021**.
- Cryostorage inventory updates on any transfer — **ELB-LOG-016**.
- Any deviation / alarm-response entry — **ELB-LOG-023**.

## 12. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

Continuous cryogenic storage and LN₂-handling safety are **guideline-anchored** (ESHRE 2015; GHS hazard classification). Values tabulated in **Appendix A (ELB-APP-A)** include: the tank level/temperature check frequency 🔧, the level and temperature alarm thresholds 🔧, the room oxygen-depletion alarm setpoint 🔧, the static-evaporation tolerance 🔧, and the maximum working fill level per tank 🔧. Each locally defined value is flagged 🔧 in Appendix A and requires Medical Director sign-off.

## 14. Teaching point

LN₂ is the most ordinary and the most dangerous substance in the laboratory: cheap, used daily, and capable of silently emptying a room of oxygen. The discipline is to treat monitoring as protection of life and of stored material at once — check the O₂ alarm before you enter, log every level so a slow leak shows as a trend rather than a surprise, and rehearse the alarm response so that, when it sounds at 2 a.m., the order is never in doubt: people first, then the tanks.
