#### Purpose

Pre-Implantation Genetic Testing (PGT) of embryos involves confirming the chromosomal status of the embryo through assessment of biopsied cells. Most miscarriages and failed implantation are believed to be due to abnormal chromosomal status. Possessing the correct chromosome complement increases the ability of an embryo to implant and result in a live birth. Historically, biopsies occurred at the cleavage stage or involved biopsy of the polar body. With more cells being able to be biopsied at the blastocyst stage, representing a smaller proportion of the embryo, blastocyst biopsy is currently regarded as being a safer procedure. Therefore, as the benefits of blastocyst biopsy became more evident, blastocyst biopsy has gradually become the method of choice in most IVF laboratories. This protocol outlines a structured methodology to train a biopsy practitioner to acquire the competency to confidently perform this procedure in a consistent manner that is safe for the embryos whilst maximising the chance of pregnancy.

#### Scope

Applicable to all trained or embryologists in training. To understand the theory behind trophectoderm biopsy and genetic testing, the preparation required for blastocyst biopsy, the equipment needed, performing the blastocyst biopsy, testing the cells for amplification and recording the overnight survival post biopsy.

#### Definitions

PGT Pre-Implantation Genetic Testing

PGT-A Pre-Implantation Genetic Testing for Aneuploidy

PGT-SR Pre-Implantation Genetic Testing for Structural Rearrangements

PGT-M Pre-Implantation Genetic Testing for Monogenic Disorders

#### Background Knowledge

All new biopsy practitioners must complete the Oxford Medical Kuwait Blastocyst Biopsy Training Workbook (TFP1-SCT-HR-0030).

To acquire the knowledge base for blastocyst biopsy, the trainee must demonstrate awareness of the concepts concerning the biology of the embryo, the equipment and consumables used as well as technical and procedural choices to ensure the safety of the embryo throughout the procedure. This information can be presented to the trainee through graphical presentations summarising the literature, videos of example biopsies and observation of real time cases. The knowledge acquisition can be assessed through essays, verbal discussions or written examination. The biopsy trainee should be able to understand and interpret the knowledge with regards to common scenarios they are likely to face as biopsy practitioners.

Examples of knowledge competencies that must be acquired include:

-   Awareness of the mandatory requirements from the HFEA in relation to embryo testing and sex selection  <!-- TODO(§8 hfea): HFEA / UK licence-condition reference — re-base to Kuwait MoH equivalent or remove (§8). NEEDS SCOTT. -->
-   understanding the benefits of trophectoderm biopsy compared to polar body and cleavage biopsy
-   understanding of the possible genetic results: euploid, aneuploid, no result, mosaics (low and high level), affected, unaffected, etc
-   understanding of the clinical implications of the possible genetic results, including the chance of pregnancy when transferring an euploid embryo or mosaic embryo, the chance of multiple pregnancy when transferring multiple euploid embryos, the chances of miscarriage when transferring an euploid embryo or mosaic embryo, etc
-   awareness that the chances of having a euploid embryo in a cycle reduces with age and increases with number of embryos biopsied.
-   understanding the importance of not disturbing the inner cell mass, and positioning the inner cell mass as far as possible from the laser shot and biopsy
-   understanding the preference of collecting several cells in order to increase the detection of mosaic embryos and that removing too many cells may be detrimental to embryo viability (optimally 5 to 8 cells)
-   ability to differentiate between a cell and a fragment, and awareness that fragments may have a different ploidy to the embryo and should therefore be avoided
-   understanding that lasers generate heat and pose a risk to cell viability. Therefore, lasers should be used conservatively and away from the inner cell mass
-   understanding the legal and ethical restrictions regarding trophectoderm biopsy in the country where the biopsy is taking place.

#### Consumables

The trainee must have an understanding of the importance of sterility for the embryo and DNase free environment for the biopsy, and that gloves must be worn throughout the procedure to avoid contaminating the biopsy. The trainee should understand the choice of consumables used for biopsy as per the Oxford Medical Kuwait PGT and Blastocyst Biopsy SOP (TFP1-SOP-EMB-0023).

#### Dish Preparation

When demonstrating competency in dish preparation for biopsy, the trainee must prepare at least 10 dishes under supervision (1 if ICSI dish preparation trained), meeting the following criteria:

-   Biopsy and tubing dishes to be made in line with Oxford Medical Kuwait PGT and Blastocyst Biopsy SOP (TFP1-SOP-EMB-0023).
-   media drops should be located centrally on the dish to ensure easy access by the angled microtools
-   dishes must be numbered with the embryo number to ensure that embryos and biopsies are traceable
-   when moving the dish between stations, the drops must not merge.

#### Equipment set-up

The trainee must demonstrate competency in using the equipment necessary for biopsy:

-   temperature of the heated stage must be constant and maintained between 36.5 and 37.5°C as measured directly in the drop
-   the inverted microscope should be configured such that the cell junctions are clearly visible
-   the holder and biopsy needle should be aligned, allowing for embryos to be held anchored to the bottom of the dish, without dragging the tools on the dish, and with the embryo, holder and biopsy needle being in the same focal plane
-   the trainee must be confident in using the micromanipulator, demonstrating control in the suction and aspiration of both the holder and biopsy needle
-   the trainee must demonstrate competence in checking the calibration of the laser and calibrating the laser if and when necessary
-   the trainee must demonstrate ability to modify the laser settings with regards to size, number of shootings and pathway.

#### Blastocyst Material for Training

Blastocysts not suitable for treatment may be used for biopsy training only when permission is given by the gamete providers via the appropriate HFEA consent forms. In addition, specific details of the type of training must also be given to the gamete providers using Oxford Medical Kuwait Unified Use of Embryos and Gametes in Training (TFP1-CON-CLN-0011) consent form. Both the TFP and HFEA consents must be in place and checked prior to use of embryos in training.  <!-- TODO(§8 hfea): HFEA / UK licence-condition reference — re-base to Kuwait MoH equivalent or remove (§8). NEEDS SCOTT. -->

The gamete providers consent to embryo storage must not be exceeded and as such the consent expiry date must remain within the patient management system and be managed as part of the ‘bring forward system’/or equivalent.

To avoid conflict of interest, the unsuitability for treatment should be determined and documented by a member of staff other than the trainee. Typical examples of types of embryos that can be used in training are 3PN blastocysts, blastocysts not suitable for embryo transfer or freezing or frozen blastocysts that patients no longer require for their own treatment.

Different embryos respond to biopsy in different ways, blastocyst biopsy may differ depending on the characteristics of each embryo. Large numbers of biopsies are therefore required in training so that the trainee may gain awareness of the expected variation in practice. This will allow the trainee to experience good and poor practice and to apply the technical skills learnt from one biopsy to another.

#### Choosing Embryos for Biopsy

The trainee must be able to competently grade blastocyst expansion, inner cell mass and trophectoderm and identify which embryos are suitable for biopsy as well as the optimal timing for biopsy. Poor quality blastocysts with an expanded blastocoel cavity (Capalbo et al., 2014) and even morulas (Kort et al., 2015) may be considered for biopsy, although blastocyst biopsy criteria must be validated onsite and may vary between clinics. Time-lapse videos of blastocyst expansion are useful tools for teaching and assessing the understanding of the local criteria in order to reach consistency between different practitioners in the same lab. The trainee should demonstrate awareness that embryos may be cultured for longer periods (even overnight) for the opportunity to increase expansion and/or trophectoderm cell number.

#### Traceability

Trainees must demonstrate competence in requesting a witness to verify that the embryo being biopsied, and the biopsy sample are traceable at all times. Biopsied blastocysts should remain in culture for at least 4 hours assess for survival and re-expansion.

#### Priming Microtools

Trainees should demonstrate competence in priming needles and holders:

-   priming the needle with media/PVP, whilst the meniscus is still visible
-   priming the holder with media
-   demonstrating fine control of the meniscus of the biopsy and holder pipettes
-   in between embryos being biopsied, emptying the biopsy needle into a spare drop, and reloading with fresh PVP (to avoid contamination between samples).

#### Positioning of Blastocyst

The trainee should be able to demonstrate sufficient control with the holder suction and aspiration to rotate the blastocyst:

-   the ICM should be located as far as possible from the biopsy location
-   the holding position would ideally have a perivitelline space between the trophectoderm cells and the zona pellucida.

#### Breaching Zona Pellucida

To reduce cell damage with the laser, the trainee should demonstrate awareness in:

-   using the laser at the correct pulse size depending on the laser used and avoiding using the laser directly on cells
-   nudging the trophectoderm with the biopsy needle to either induce collapse or create a perivitelline gap
-   staggering the zona pellucida following a small slit in the zona pellucida to create an artificial perivitelline gap

The trainee should have an awareness that in the event that the laser is malfunctioning or not available, biopsy may be performed by a highly experienced biopsy practitioner in hatching blasts if trophectoderm is hemorrhaging at a distance from the ICM. Alternatively, embryo biopsy should not take place and embryos cryopreserved for a later biopsy if required.

#### Picking up Cells

The trainee should demonstrate competence in picking up cells using the biopsy needle through a controlled gentle suction:

-   ICM cells should not be touched by the biopsy needle
-   Cells should be counted as they enter the biopsy needle (5 to 8 cells)
-   Suction should be controlled to avoid cell lysis
-   Whilst aspirating cells into the biopsy needle, the biopsy needle should be gently pressed against the embryo to avoid lysing the cells during suction
-   Suction pressure should be reduced from the biopsy needle to allow for lasering of the cell junctions

#### Applying Laser on Cell junction

The trainee should demonstrate:

-   ability of applying laser pulses on cell junctions whilst applying appropriate pressure on the cells
-   minimum number of pulses used (aiming for three) although the number of pulses will differ depending on the length of the laser shoot
-   awareness that the heat generated by the laser might compromise cell viability in the embryo and/or DNA integrity in the biopsy
-   lasering in the correct focal plane to ensure accuracy of the laser pulse
-   lasering away from the inner cell mass
-   critical assessment of the number of embryos to be biopsied given the quality of the blastocyst.

#### Disconnecting Biopsied Cell from Embryo

Different techniques are available to approach the trophectoderm biopsy:

1\. Locate cells to biopsy at 3 o’clock and use laser and aspiration control to release the cells.

2\. Mechanical biopsy consists of a rapid flick between the holding and the biopsy pipettes as the biopsy pipette holds the cells.

3\. The Oxford Medical Kuwait preferred method is a combination of approximately three laser shots to loosen cells from the trophectoderm and complete detachment with the flicking technique.

#### Post Biopsy

Once the biopsy is detached from the embryo, the trainee should demonstrate competence in:

-   Positioning the embryo and the biopsy sample at opposite sites in the dish.
-   Determining whether the biopsy cells are intact (membrane visible) or lysed and recording on the biopsy form
-   Returning embryo to a post-biopsy dish prior to cryopreservation (if applicable).
-   Washing the biopsy sample through buffer drops and then loading into a labelled tube.
-   Visualising the biopsy inside the tube. The tubing procedure is best supervised using a screen connected to the microscope, so that the supervisor can also visualise the biopsied cells entering the tube.
-   Placing the tube in a cooled tube rack to preserve DNA integrity.
-   Storing the samples prior to collection in a -20°C freezer.

#### Progression to Clinical Cases

The trainee will be signed off competent when at least 20 trophectoderm samples have been sent for DNA amplification, with a ≥95% amplification rate and 0% contamination rate from 20 negative controls. At least 30 biopsied embryos should be cultured further to assess re-expansion and at least 95% of blastocysts should survive overnight. Time-lapse is a useful tool to assess survival and re-expansion rates. Re-biopsy of surviving blastocyst may be attempted, although survival rates are likely to be reduced compared with previously biopsied embryos.

Once these target thresholds are met, the trainee may progress to splitting clinical cases where there are at least 4 embryos suitable for biopsy. Biopsy cases should be split for as long as deemed necessary by the supervisor. The KPIs are monitored continuously and regularly.

#### Trouble Shooting Suboptimal KPI

Normal performance should be determined based on supervision at the time of biopsy and retrospective video assessment. To be signed off, the trainee should meet the following KPI thresholds:

-   the time length of the biopsy should be less than 3 minutes per sample in straight forward cases;
-   ≥95% overnight re-expansion rate
-   ≥95% of biopsies should amplify
-   the biopsy should consist of between 5 to 8 cells

Once an indicator is identified as being sub-optimal, a corrective action must be implemented in order to achieve improvement in the quality of care. Evaluation of videos of biopsy procedures may help identify the appropriate corrective action during training and after being signed off.

In the event of low survival/re-expansion rate (<95%) the following corrective actions are applicable:

Review of biopsy videos to assess:

-   aspiration control
-   a maximum of 8 cells are collected from a single blastocyst
-   timing of blastocyst biopsy in terms of expansion
-   vibration during biopsy
-   the size of the zona pellucida breach was not too large or too small
-   whether use of laser was excessive
-   whether ICM was inappropriately disrupted (mechanically or by laser pulses)
-   whether blastocyst was firmly held during biopsy, without spinning
-   whether environmental conditions are embryo-safe (i.e. toxicity, temperature, air quality, light)
-   whether micro-tools are correctly angled and aligned

In the event of high rate of no PCR amplification (>5%), the following corrective actions are applicable:

-   Review the tubing technique to ensure biopsied cells are correctly washed and visualised within the tube
-   Review the biopsy videos to assess:
    -   suction power at the time of pulling the cells from the embryo as this could lyse the cells with the consequent DNA lost
    -   length and the quantity of laser pulses (excessive use of laser may compromise DNA integrity in the biopsy
    -   assessment of biopsy integrity
    -   lasering only cell junctions (lasering cells should be avoided).

#### Conclusion

Trophectoderm biopsy is a technically demanding procedure which requires well trained embryologists. Following this structured training protocol, the trainee would gain experience and confidence in a closely monitored program.
