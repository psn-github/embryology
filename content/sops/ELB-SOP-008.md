## 1. Purpose

To define the routine for opening, preparing, running and closing the Embryology Laboratory each working day, so that the culture environment, equipment and consumables are verified ready before any gamete or embryo is handled, and are left secure and monitored at the end of the day. A disciplined open/close routine is the first line of defence for embryo safety: most environmental excursions are caught at the daily check, not during a procedure.

## 2. Scope

Applies to every working day in the Embryology Laboratory, including days with no scheduled cases (storage and monitoring duties still apply). Covers the andrology and embryology work areas, incubators, workstations, cryostorage monitoring and the laboratory environment. Applies to the patient couple's own gametes and embryos only (autologous); donor and surrogacy work is not performed.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Opening embryologist** — performs and records the opening checks before any case begins; escalates any out-of-range result before work starts.
- **Embryologists on shift** — maintain the environment during the day and complete in-day monitoring at the defined intervals.
- **Closing embryologist** — performs the end-of-day checks, confirms storage and monitoring are secure, and records closure.
- **Laboratory Lead / Quality Manager** — reviews the daily records, trends the data and authorises any deviation handling.

## 4. Definitions & abbreviations

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| QC | Quality control |
| TVOC | Total volatile organic compounds (air-quality measure) |
| BMS | Building management / environmental alarm system |

## 5. Equipment, materials & media

- Incubators (benchtop and box), CO₂/O₂ supply and gas analyser.
- Independent reference thermometers and the continuous monitoring/alarm system.
- Laminar flow hoods and workstations (heated stages).
- Air-quality / TVOC monitor (ELB-SOP-013).
- LN₂ storage tanks and their level/temperature monitoring (ELB-SOP-037).
- Day's media and consumables, within shelf life, equilibrated per the relevant IFU.

> **IFU cross-reference.** Media and dishes are equilibrated for the time, temperature and gas conditions stated in the manufacturer IFU and **Appendix A** before use. Do not begin a case on dishes that have not reached equilibration.

## 6. Pre-procedure checks

1. Confirm the laboratory was secure overnight and no environmental alarm is active or unacknowledged.
2. Confirm utilities: mains and backup power, gas cylinder/line pressures, and LN₂ supply adequate for the day.
3. Confirm the air handling is running and the TVOC reading is within the locally defined range 🔧 (**Appendix A**, ELB-SOP-013).

## 7. Procedure

**Opening**

1. Record date, time and operator on the daily record.
2. Verify each incubator temperature, CO₂ (and O₂ where used) against the independent reference and the setpoint (ELB-SOP-039); record on the incubator QC log (ELB-LOG-018).
3. Confirm the monitoring/alarm system is armed and logging, with no overnight excursion outstanding.
4. Check LN₂ tank levels/temperatures are within range (ELB-SOP-037); record.
5. Switch on and confirm laminar flow hoods and heated stages reach setpoint (ELB-SOP-044, **Appendix A**).
6. Read and record the air-quality / TVOC value (ELB-LOG-022).
7. Confirm the day's media and consumables are in date, sufficient and equilibrating; record lot numbers as cases require.
8. Confirm no opening check is out of range before any oocyte, sperm or embryo is removed from storage.

**During the day**

9. Maintain workstation cleanliness and sterile technique (ELB-SOP-009).
10. Repeat environmental monitoring at the locally defined in-day intervals 🔧 (**Appendix A**).

**Closing**

11. Confirm all gametes and embryos are returned to the correct, labelled incubator/storage position and witnessed (ELB-SOP-002).
12. Confirm incubators and tanks are within range and the alarm system is armed for the unattended period.
13. Power down or set hoods/stages per the IFU; dispose of waste (ELB-SOP-015).
14. Record closure time and operator; note anything for the next shift.

## 8. Critical control points & witnessing

| Checkpoint | What is verified | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **W1** | All gametes/embryos accounted for and in the correct labelled position at close | Second-person / RI Witness against inventory |

> **CRITICAL — do not start work on a failed open.** If any opening check (temperature, gas, pH-relevant gas, LN₂, air quality) is out of range, hold all clinical work, escalate, and resolve before removing any material from storage.

## 9. Acceptance / rejection criteria

The laboratory is "ready to run" only when every opening parameter is within its setpoint/tolerance (**Appendix A**) and monitoring is confirmed active. Any single out-of-range parameter is a reject condition until corrected and re-verified.

## 10. Deviations

Any failed check, environmental excursion or equipment fault is managed under the deviation and CAPA policy (ELB-POL-006) and recorded in the deviation log (ELB-LOG-023). Events that may affect material already in culture or storage are escalated immediately.

## 11. Records generated

- Incubator QC log — **ELB-LOG-018**.
- Air quality / TVOC log — **ELB-LOG-022**.
- LN₂ fill / tank monitoring log — **ELB-LOG-021**.
- Calibration log entries where checks fall due — **ELB-LOG-020**.
- Any deviation entry — **ELB-LOG-023**.

## 12. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

Check intervals (opening, in-day, closing), incubator and stage tolerances, the air-quality / TVOC acceptable range 🔧 and LN₂ alarm thresholds 🔧 are recorded in **Appendix A**. Each is marked guideline-anchored (ESHRE 2015) or locally defined (🔧, requiring Medical Director sign-off).

## 14. Teaching point

The opening check is the cheapest error you will ever catch. A drifting incubator or a failed gas line found at 07:30, before any dish leaves storage, costs a few minutes; found mid-case it can cost a cohort. Treat the daily routine as a procedure in its own right — recorded, witnessed at close, and never skipped on a "quiet" day.
