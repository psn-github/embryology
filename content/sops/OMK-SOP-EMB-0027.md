## Purpose

To describe the time points at which embryos are graded and the associated grading schemes used to grade embryos. To define the process of embryo grading at cleavage stage and Day 5/6 of development.

## Scope

Applicable to all laboratory staff deemed competent to perform embryo grading.

## Definitions

TL Time Lapse

EQA External Quality Assurance

PTMRIE Polish Society for Reproductive Medicine and Embryology

### 5.2 Equipment Checks

Prior to observing embryos, the equipment to be used must be checked to ensure it is operating optimally.

The flowhood must be checked to ensure the flow is on and the hood is clear of used dishes/pipettes and tubes.

Ensure the RI heated stage/Inverted or Stereo Microscope is switched on and at the correct set point.

Ensure the embryos in the wells of the TL dish are clearly visible.

### 5.4 Embryo Grading Procedure

For standard culture, consideration must be given to maintaining temperature, pH and osmolality during the embryo grading procedure, therefore oil covered dishes are used. The procedure should be performed as quickly as practically possible to minimise the exposure of the embryos outside the incubator environment.

Embryo grading must be performed at x200-x400 magnification in order to be able to observe the necessary features of the embryo to allocate a grade accurately.

The aim for all patients is to culture embryos until Day 5 and perform embryo transfer at that stage.

Not all patients will have embryos suitable for transfer on Day 5 and patients must therefore be counselled about this risk of cancellation (on average approximately 5%).

Embryos that are cleavage stage on Day 5 or 6 are not suitable for embryo transfer.

In the event that no embryos are suitable on Day 5 i.e. the embryos are all still at cleavage stage, either cancel the embryo transfer or culture until day 6 and reassess embryo development at that stage.

In the event that the patient has a low number of embryos, or the embryo quality is low, a discussion must be had with the patient explaining the benefits and risks of day 5 culture. The embryologist and patient must then decide the most suitable way forward.

For detailed grading on Day 2 or 3 i.e. for TL patients or patients having a Day 2/3 embryo transfer, use the grading scheme below to grade the embryos.

Note the cell number alongside separate grades for cellular regularity and degree of fragmentation. Cellular regularity and fragmentation are graded on a scale of 1-4 with a grade 4 denoting the highest quality embryo.

e.g. 5/4/2 5 cell, regular cells, 20-50% fragmentation

Regularity is graded in a stage specific way i.e. a 3-cell embryo with one large cell and two small cells would be regarded as ‘regular’.

Also note the presence of any vacuoles, granularity, reverse cleavage, direct cleavage and multi-nucleated cells.

When selecting embryos for transfer, preference must be given to the embryo(s) with the highest grade and those on target in terms of their development. For clinics using TL, KID scores for example should also be considered.

Embryos with correct number of cells for their age with regularity and fragmentation scores of 3 or 4 are considered suitable for cryopreservation on Day 2/3.

Embryos graded A, B or C on Day 5/6 are considered suitable for cryopreservation.

Embryos graded E-F on Day 5 should be cultured until Day 6 and then re-assessed for suitability for cryopreservation. Culture to and cryopreservation on Day 7, is at the local clinic’s discretion.

_Note: In Poland embryos graded D may also be considered for cryopreservation. In addition, the PTMRIE grade must also be recorded in the laboratory notes._

All clinics must participate in the Oxford Medical Kuwait EQA scheme and review their clinical practice in line with the results.

## Standards/Key Performance/Quality Indicators

% Patients having Day 5 Embryo Transfer ≥90%

% Blastocyst Formation Day 5 ≥40%

% Grade A or B Blastocysts Day 5 ≥30%

% Gold Standard Match on EQA ≥70%

## Monitoring

The Oxford Medical Kuwait External Quality Assurance (EQA) scheme is distributed three times per year and assesses individual operators’ agreement with the gold standard grade. Any operators with <70% agreement are required to regrade the embryos following discussion with their laboratory manager.

Results of the Oxford Medical Kuwait EQA scheme are discussed at each quarterly Head of Laboratories meeting.

A Oxford Medical Kuwait Laboratory Manager is required to audit this SOP annually.
