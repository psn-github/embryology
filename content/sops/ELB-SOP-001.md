## 1. Purpose

To define how laboratory records are created, completed, corrected, controlled, stored and retained, so that every gamete and embryo handled in the laboratory has a complete, legible, attributable and traceable record. Reliable records are the foundation of identity assurance, deviation management and the quality management system; this SOP makes recordkeeping uniform across all activities.

## 2. Scope

Applies to every controlled document, log, worksheet and form generated in the Embryology Laboratory for procedures performed on the **patient couple's own gametes and embryos** (autologous only). Covers paper and electronic records, including the RI Witness electronic record (ELB-SOP-002). Donor and surrogacy records are out of scope and are not held. The clinical medical record is owned by the treating service; this SOP governs the laboratory record only.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **All laboratory staff** — complete records contemporaneously at the point of work, sign or initial their own entries, and never record an action they did not perform or witness.
- **Performing embryologist** — owns the completeness and accuracy of the records for the cycles they handle.
- **Witness** — signs only what they have personally verified (ELB-SOP-002).
- **Quality Manager** — controls document issue and revision, audits record completeness, and manages archiving and retention.
- **Medical Director** — approves controlled documents and owns the locally defined retention period (**Appendix A**).

## 4. Definitions & abbreviations

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Controlled document | A versioned SOP, policy, form, log or appendix issued and revised under document control |
| Contemporaneous | Recorded at the time the action is performed, not reconstructed afterwards |
| Attributable | Each entry is traceable to the named person who made it |
| ALCOA | Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, Accurate — the data-integrity principles |
| EW | Electronic witnessing (RI Witness) |

## 5. Equipment, materials & media

- Controlled paper forms and logs printed from the current issued version only.
- The laboratory information system and the RI Witness electronic record, each access-controlled and backed up.
- Permanent (non-erasable) ink for paper entries; controlled rubber stamps where used.
- Secure, environmentally suitable archive storage.

> **IFU cross-reference.** Electronic systems are operated and backed up per the vendor instructions. Do not store original records in conditions outside the manufacturer or archive specification.

## 6. Pre-procedure checks

1. Confirm the form or log in use is the **current issued version** (check docId and version against the register).
2. Confirm the patient and cycle identifiers are correct before any entry (ELB-SOP-016).
3. For electronic records, confirm you are logged in under your own credentials; never work under another person's login.
4. Confirm any superseded paper version has been withdrawn from circulation.

> **CRITICAL — attribution.** Every record entry is made by, and attributable to, the person who performed the work. Do not pre-sign, back-date, or complete a record on another person's behalf.

## 7. Procedure

1. Record each action **contemporaneously**, at the point of work, in permanent ink or directly in the validated electronic system.
2. Identify the patient/cycle on every page or screen before entering data (ELB-SOP-016).
3. Enter complete data: what was done, when, with which materials (lot/expiry), the result, and any deviation.
4. Sign or initial and date each entry. For witnessed steps, ensure the witness signs per ELB-SOP-002.
5. **To correct an error**, draw a single line through the incorrect entry so it remains legible, write the correction adjacent, and initial and date the change. Never overwrite, erase, obliterate or use correction fluid.
6. For electronic corrections, use the system's audit-tracked amendment function; the original value and the reason remain visible.
7. Do not leave mandatory fields blank. If a field does not apply, mark it "N/A"; if data is unavailable, record why.
8. At the end of the cycle, assemble the records, verify completeness against the laboratory cycle checklist (ELB-FORM-005), and file them under the patient/cycle identifier.
9. Archive completed records to secure storage for the retention period (**Appendix A** 🔧).

## 8. Critical control points & witnessing

Identity confirmation on a record is itself a witnessed control (ELB-SOP-002). The record is the evidence that the witness checkpoint occurred.

| Checkpoint | What is verified | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **W1** | Patient/cycle identifiers on the record match the material in hand before any entry | EW match (RI Witness) + label read, or second-person check |
| **W2** | Witnessed procedural steps carry both operator and witness attribution | Co-signature per ELB-SOP-002 |

> **CRITICAL CONTROL POINT.** A record without correct identity attribution is not valid. If identity cannot be confirmed, stop, resolve identity, and only then record.

## 9. Acceptance / rejection criteria

A record is **acceptable** when it is complete, legible, attributable, contemporaneous, and free of obscured corrections. A record is **rejected** (treated as a deviation) when it has blank mandatory fields, illegible or obliterated entries, missing signatures, or a version mismatch. Rejected records are not destroyed; they are corrected under Section 7 and the gap is logged.

## 10. Deviations

A missing, incomplete, illegible or non-attributable record is managed under the deviation and CAPA policy (ELB-POL-006) and entered in the deviation log (ELB-LOG-023). A documentation gap that affects identity or consent is escalated and may be a reportable adverse event (ELB-POL-005).

## 11. Records generated

- This SOP governs the format and control of all suite logs and forms rather than generating a single record of its own.
- Document-control register entries (issue, revision, withdrawal).
- Any deviation entry — **ELB-LOG-023**.

## 14. Teaching point

In an IVF laboratory the record is not paperwork that follows the work — it *is* part of the work. A gamete with an incomplete record is a gamete whose identity, consent and handling can no longer be proven, and an embryo's traceability cannot be reconstructed after the fact. Record at the moment of action, attribute it to yourself, and correct errors transparently: legibility and attribution are what make every later check possible.
