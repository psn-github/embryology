## Purpose

To describe the procedure for analysing semen parameters and preparing the semen sample for IUI, IVF or ICSI treatment. Semen samples must be prepared prior to use in treatment. There are several different preparation methods, and these should be applied to different samples depending on their concentration and motility parameters.

## Scope

Applicable to all embryologists, andrologists and practitioners deemed competent to perform semen analysis and preparation:

Gamete/embryo donation and surrogacy are out of scope (prohibited in Kuwait).

## Definitions

IUI Intra Uterine Insemination

IVF In Vitro Fertilisation

ICSI Intra Cytoplasmic Sperm Injection

D.O.B. Date of Birth

### 5.3 Witnessing Checks (Full Name, Clinic Number & D.O.B.)

The sample pot must be labelled with three identifiers, the patient’s full name, clinic number and D.O.B.

Hand the patient the sample pot and provide the patient with sample production instructions. Ask the patient to initial the sample pot/sign the production form.

Record the number of day’s abstinence and any illness/medication in last 2-3 months if not recorded on the sample production form. Instruct the patient to inform the laboratory team if not all the sample is collected.

If the sample has been produced off site, on arrival at the clinic ask the patient to state their full name and D.O.B. Check this information along with their clinic number matches the patient’s laboratory record and sample pot. Sign for these checks in the laboratory record. Ensure the patient has completed the off-site production declaration form.

Once the sample has been produced, transfer the sample to the flowhood.

Only one sample may be processed in a flowhood at any one time.

Samples that are incorrectly labelled, unlabelled or whereby there is doubt as to its identification must be rejected. If the name and D.O.B. on the sample pot and production form do not match the sample must be rejected. ### 5.4 Sample Production

Semen samples should where possible be produced on site. If the patient is unable to produce on site, offer the patient the option to produce off site. Provide the patient with a labelled sample pot. If the sample is produced off-site the patient must sign an off-site production declaration form stating the date/time/place of production and that the sample is their own and has not been tampered with. Advise the patient to keep the sample warm and to bring it to the clinic within 50 minutes of production.

Patients may also be given the option of producing the sample into a non-toxic condom.

If the patient is to produce on site, the sample production room must be cleaned prior to production, by wiping all surfaces (including door handles, the sample production hatch, chairs, pens etc.) in the production room using alcohol free disinfectant wipes/Oosafe.

All patients must be triaged for Covid-19 before attending the clinic.

### 5.5 Preparation of Density Gradients & Test Tubes for Sample Washing

Prepare 80% and 40% PureSperm Gradients in 10ml aliquots in conical tubes.

80% PureSperm: Mix 8ml of Puresperm100 with 2ml of GIVF+ in a conical tube. Gently invert to mix.

40% PureSperm: Mix 4ml of Puresperm100 with 6ml of GIVF+ in a conical tube. Gently invert to mix

To prepare larger aliquots of 80% and 40% gradients, use the same dilution factor as described above.

To prepare a two-step gradient, place 1ml of 80% PureSperm into a conical tube, gently layer on top a further 1ml of 40% PureSperm. Take care not to disturb the interface between the two layers. Allow to warm to room temperature prior to use.

Alternatively, for preparation of samples with <2M/ml and <5% motility, a one-step gradient can be used consisting of 1ml of 40% PureSperm. Place 1ml of 40% PureSperm in a conical tube and allow to warm to room temperature.

Prepare conical tubes for washing, place 2ml of GIVF+ in one tube (Wash/Stock tube) plus a further 4mls of GIVF+ in a second conical tube (Media tube) for the 2nd wash and to resuspend the pellet at the end of the preparation procedure. Equilibrate in a gassed incubator for a minimum of 6 hours. Tightly cap the tubes when they are removed from the incubator ready for use.

### 5.6 Timing of Sperm Preparation Procedure

Begin preparation of the semen sample 15-60 minutes post-production. If the sample is viscous or fails to liquefy, GIVF+ can be added to the sample or the sample can be syringed/warmed.

**5.7 Centrifugation Speeds and Timings**

PureSperm Gradient 15-20 minutes at 0.3 rcf

Wash Steps 5 minutes at 0.3 rcf

High Speed Spin 5 minutes at 0.5-0.8 rcf

Swim-Up 60-90 minutes in a gassed incubator

### 5.8 Semen Analysis

Assess the volume, concentration and motility of the initial semen sample.

Assessments of anti-sperm antibodies, pH, morphology and viability are all optional depending on the patient’s clinical history or sample type.

The initial parameters of the sample are used to determine the most suitable method of sperm preparation.

Post preparation, note the volume of the final stock solution and assess concentration and motility.

The initial and post preparation parameters are used to determine whether IVF or ICSI is the most suitable treatment type. See TFP1-POL-EM-0001 Oxford Medical Kuwait Criteria for IVF or ICSI.

Gloves must be worn when handling human semen.

#### 5.8.1 Assessment of Sample Volume (Reference Value ≥1.4ml)

Using a serological pipette, thoroughly mix the sample by pipetting up and down several times and then measure the total volume of the sample using the graduations on the pipette.

#### 5.8.2 Assessment of Concentration (Reference Value ≥16M/ml)

Mix 10µl of neat semen with 190µl of sterile water in an Eppendorf tube. Vortex to mix thoroughly. Attach the cover slip #4 or 0.44mm to the haemocytometer until Newtons Rings are visible. Gently pipette approximately 8-10 µl of the diluted sample into each side of the haemocytometer chamber. Allow the chamber to settle for 4 minutes in a humid chamber before analysis.

Observe the chamber at X200-400 magnification. The chamber consists of two grids with 25 large squares on each. Depending on the concentration of the sperm in the sample, count either the number of whole sperm in 5 large squares per grid or 25 large squares per grid. Determine the concentration of the sample in M/ml.

#### 5.8.3 Assessment of Motility (Reference Value A + B= ≥30%)

Place 10 µl of semen onto a clear glass slide and cover with a glass cover slip. Observe the slide at X200-400 magnification. Immediately following slide preparation, grade the motility of 100-200 sperm using the following criteria:

**Grade A** – rapid progressive motility

**Grade B** – slow or sluggish motility

**Grade C** – non-progressive motility

**Grade D** – immotile

Note the results as a percentage of each grade.

#### 5.8.4 Assessment of Morphology, if Indicated (Reference Value normal forms ≥4%)

Place 5µl of semen onto a pre-stained morphology slide. Allow the slide to air dry for 15-30 minutes. Observe the slide at X1000 magnification under oil immersion. Grade 100-200 whole sperm as ‘normal’ or ‘abnormal’ according to WHO 2021 criteria. Note the result as a percentage of normal forms.

For sperm to be considered normal both the tail and head must be normal. All borderline sperm must be considered abnormal.

Normal sperm have smooth, oval shaped head with a well-defined acrosomal area comprising 40-70% of the head area. The acrosomal area should not contain any large vacuoles and not more than two small vacuoles. There should be no vacuoles in the post acrosomal region.

The midpiece should be slender and regular and approximately the same length as the head. The axis of the midpiece should be aligned with the axis of the sperm head. Cytoplasmic droplets should be < 1/3 of the size of the sperm head.

The tail should be regular and thinner than the midpiece and around ten times the length of the head.

#### 5.8.5 Assessment of Anti-Sperm Antibodies IgG, if Indicated (Reference Value <50%)

Allow the reagents to warm to room temperature.

Thoroughly mix the reagent(s). Add 5µl of each reagent to a clear glass slide along with 5µl of semen. Mix on the slide using the pipette tip. Place a cover slip on top.

Allow the sample to settle for 3 minutes then observe the slide under X200-400 magnification.

Assess 100 motile sperm for the presence of beads bound to them. Convert the number of sperm showing evidence of binding to a percentage.

If the initial assessment shows no binding, re-assess in 10 minutes.

#### 5.8.6 Assessment of pH, if Indicated (Reference pH ≥7.2)

Perform the pH assessment within 1 hour of sample production.

Place a droplet of neat semen onto pH paper. Wait for the colour of the impregnated zone to become uniform. Compare the colour to the calibration strip and read the pH.

#### 5.8.7 Assessment of Viability, Performed if 0% Motility (Reference Value ≥54%)

SpermMobil can be used to initiate motility in viable non-motile sperm. The process for using SpermMobil is outlined in the Oxford Medical Kuwait ICSI SOP.

Alternatively, the Hypo-Osmotic Swelling Test (HOS) may be used to distinguish between viable and non-viable non-motile sperm.

### 5.9 Sperm Preparation

#### 5.9.1 80:40% Density Gradient (IUI, ICSI or IVF Samples)

**_Density Gradient - 1st Wash – 2nd Wash - Stock_**

Prepare samples of ≥2M/ml and ≥5% motility via 2-step density gradient.

Gently layer 1-3ml of semen on top of the density gradient (for IUI the whole sample should be prepared, additional density gradients may be required). Centrifuge at 0.3 r.c.f. for 15-20 minutes.

Following centrifugation move the pellet(s) to the wash/stock tube. Mix thoroughly. Centrifuge at 0.3 r.c.f. for 5 minutes (1st wash).

Following the 1st wash, remove the supernatant. Add 2mls of GIVF+ and mix thoroughly. Centrifuge at 0.3 r.c.f. for 5 minutes (2nd wash).

Following the 2nd wash, remove the supernatant and resuspend the pellet in GIVF+. For IUI/IVF resuspend in 0.5-1ml. For ICSI samples resuspend in 0.1-1.0ml (depending on the concentration) of GIVF+. This is the final stock solution.

Assess the concentration and motility of the final stock solution as described above. Determine the samples suitability for IVF or ICSI using the Oxford Medical Kuwait Criteria for IVF or ICSI (TFP1-POL-EMB-0001).

Clear the flowhood of any used consumables. Use Oosafe spray ± sterile water to clean the flowhood.

#### 5.9.2 40% Density Gradient (ICSI Samples)

**_Density Gradient - 1st Wash – 2nd Wash - Stock_**

Preparation of samples with <2M/ml and <5% motility via 1-step density gradient.

Gently layer 1-2ml of semen on top of the density gradient. Centrifuge at 0.3 r.c.f. for 15-20 minutes.

Following centrifugation move the pellet(s) to the wash/stock tube. Mix thoroughly. Centrifuge at 0.3 r.c.f. for 5 minutes (1st wash).

Following the 1st wash, remove the supernatant. Add 2mls of GIVF+ and mix thoroughly. Centrifuge at 0.3 r.c.f. for 5 minutes (2nd wash).

Following the 2nd wash, remove the supernatant and resuspend the pellet in GIVF+. Resuspend in 0.1-0.5ml of GIVF+ in preparation for ICSI. This is the final stock solution.

Assess the concentration and motility of the final stock solution as described above.

Clear the flowhood of any used consumables. Use Oosafe spray ± sterile water to clean the flowhood.


**_1st Wash – 2nd Wash - Stock_**


Transfer the semen sample to the Wash/Stock tube containing 2mls of GIVF+. Mix thoroughly. Centrifuge at 0.3 r.c.f. for 5 minutes (1st wash).

Following the 1st wash, remove the supernatant. Add 2mls of GIVF+ and mix thoroughly. Centrifuge at 0.3 r.c.f. for 5 minutes (2nd wash).

Following the 2nd wash, remove the supernatant and resuspend the pellet in 0.1-0.5ml of GIVF+ depending on the final concentration. For IUI resuspend in 0.5ml-1ml. This is the final stock solution.

Assess the concentration and motility of the final stock solution as described above.

Clear the flowhood of any used consumables. Use Oosafe spray ± sterile water to clean the flowhood.

#### 5.9.4 High Speed Wash Only (ICSI Samples Only)

**1st Wash – 2nd Wash - Stock**

Prepare samples with only occasional sperm via ‘high speed wash only’.

Transfer the semen/SSR sample to the Wash/Stock tube containing 2mls of GIVF+.

Mix thoroughly. Centrifuge at 0.5-0.8 r.c.f. for 5 minutes (1st wash).

Following the 1st wash, remove the supernatant. Add 2mls of GIVF+ and mix thoroughly. Centrifuge at 0.5-0.8 r.c.f. for 5 minutes (2nd wash).

Following the 2nd wash, remove the supernatant and resuspend the pellet in 0.1-0.5ml of GIVF+ depending on the final concentration. This is the final stock solution.

Assess the concentration and motility of the final stock solution as described above or by assessment in a ‘lake dish’.

Clear the flowhood of any used consumables. Use Oosafe spray ± sterile water to clean the flowhood.

#### 5.9.5 Swim-Up (IUI/IVF Samples)

**1st Wash – Swim-Up – 2nd Wash - Stock**

Swim-ups can be used as an alternative to density gradients to prepare samples with normal motility and concentration.

Transfer the semen sample to the Wash tube and centrifuge at 0.3 r.c.f. for 5 minutes (1st wash).

Following the 1st wash, remove the supernatant. Gently disturb the pellet using a glass pipette. Gently layer on top 2mls GIVF+. Incubate the sample in a gassed incubator at a 45-degree angle for 60-90 minutes.

Following incubation, remove the supernatant and transfer to the Wash/Stock Tube. Centrifuge at 0.3 r.c.f. for 5 minutes.

Following the 2nd wash, remove the supernatant and resuspend the pellet in GIVF+. For IUI/IVF resuspend in 0.5-1ml.

Assess the concentration and motility of the final stock solution as described above. Determine the samples suitability for IVF or ICSI using the Oxford Medical Kuwait Criteria for IVF & ICSI(TFP1-POL-EMB-0001).

Clear the flowhood of any used consumables. Use Oosafe spray ± sterile water to clean the flowhood.


Before beginning the thawing process perform local checks to ensure that the thaw is being conducted on the correct date, the correct sample is being thawed and oocytes have been successfully retrieved for IVF/ICSI patients.

Determine the number of straws/ampoules to thaw. If more than one ejaculate has been frozen, identify the correct ejaculate to be thawed.

Sperm thawing requires a second witness to be used.

**For patient samples**, alongside a second witness, check the patient’s full name, clinic number, D.O.B and freeze date on the Visi/Cassette, ampoules/straws matches that in the laboratory notes.

In addition, again alongside a second witness, check the test tubes for preparation are labelled with the patient and partner’s (if applicable) full name, clinic number and D.O.B. and this information matches that in the laboratory notes and RI Witness.




Allow to warm to room temperature.

Transfer the sample to a labelled conical tube. Add an equivalent volume of equilibrated GIVF+ to the thawed sample dropwise. GIVF+ must be added slowly in order to gradually adjust the osmolality of the sample.

Remove 50µl of the post thaw sample and place in an Eppendorf tube for subsequent concentration and motility assessment.

The thawed sample can then be prepared using one of the methods above based on the thawed samples concentration and motility.

### 5.11 Surgically Retrieved Sperm (SSR)

GMOPS+ supplemented with heparin is used for the SSR procedure, (20µl Heparin is added to each 12.5ml tube of GMOPS+).

Prior to the SSR procedure and alongside a second witness, ask the patient to state their full name and D.O.B. Check this information along with their clinic number matches the patient’s laboratory record and RI Witness card. Sign for these checks in the laboratory record. Place the RI card in the reader area.

Check the patient’s full name, clinic number and D.O.B. on the dishes matches that in the laboratory notes and RI Witness. If the SSR procedure is taking place ‘off site’ it may not be possible to use RI Witness, in which case a second witness should verify the details on the dishes.

Collect PESA/MESA sperm into a 0.5ml centre well dish containing 0.5ml of GMOPS+. Prepare either via ‘wash only’ or ‘density gradient’ as described above.

Collect biopsy samples into a 60mm culture dish containing 5mls of GMOPS+. Extract the sperm from the tubules by emptying the tubules via gradual pressure along the length of the tubule or macerating the tubules.

Maceration can be performed by placing a cell strainer over a centre well dish containing 0.5ml GMOPS+. A 5ml test tube is then used to macerate and force the sperm through the cell strainer and into the centre well dish.

Once all the tissue has been processed, collect the fluid and transfer to 1-2 conical tubes. Centrifuge at 0.3-0.8 r.c.f. for 5 minutes.

If the sample is for freezing, follow the Sperm Freezing/Thawing for Patients Own Treatment SOP (TFP1-SOP-AND-3).

If the sample is for immediate treatment, prepare following the ‘High Speed Wash Only’ or ‘Wash Only’ method described above.

### 5.12 Retrograde Ejaculation

All receptacles/tubes used during the processing of a retrograde sample must be labelled with the patient’s full name, clinic number and D.O.B. Witnessing should be untaken as described in 5.3 with the extra receptacles included.

Retrograde samples must be produced on site so that the sample can be prepared as quickly as possible.

The patient is required to adjust the osmolality and pH of their urine prior to sperm production.

Advise the patient to take 2 Alka-Seltzer tablets or a 20ml Potassium Citrate mixture the night before producing the sample and again 2-4 hours prior to sample production.

On the morning of sample production ask the patient to avoid fruit juice/caffeine and drink approximately 500ml of water.

Immediately prior to sample production the patient must empty their bladder. Sample production must follow immediately afterwards.

Advise the patient to collect any ejaculate produced into the semen pot and then empty their bladder and collect the urine into the sterile jug provided.

Both semen and urine samples must be passed to the laboratory for immediate analysis and preparation.

If an adequate number of motile sperm are in the semen sample for treatment proceed with preparing the semen sample alone.

If no sperm or an inadequate number are found in the semen sample proceed with processing the urine sample. Determine the pH of the sample.

Transfer the urine to pre-labelled conical tubes and centrifuge at 0.3-0.8 r.c.f. for 5 minutes.

Remove the pellet and re-suspend in 0.5ml of GIVF+. Assess the suspension for the presence of sperm. If sperm are found, prepare the sample via density gradient or ‘wash only’ as described above.

### 5.13 Calculation of Insemination Values

Equilibrate the stock solution in a gassed incubator for 30-120 minutes prior to insemination.

The standard number of motile sperm for insemination is 75,000/0.7ml well.

The concentration and motility of the final sperm stock sample is used to calculate the number of microliters of sperm to add to each well.

The final sperm concentration is multiplied by the number of motile sperm (grade A + B, do not include grade C) to give the total motile count (TMC). This figure is then cross referenced to the insemination chart to provide the volume of sperm that needs to be added to each well to give 75,000 motile sperm.

If a patient has previously had a high number of 3PN’s or a low number of 2PN’s with IVF, the insemination number can be increased or decreased accordingly for specific patient cases. Note: if the number of motile sperm used for insemination is other than 75,000 (as indicated as above) then the insemination concentration calculation must be adjusted accordingly.

See appendix 1 for the insemination chart for insemination at 75,000 motile sperm/0.7ml well.

Example for insemination at 75,000 motile sperm/0.7ml well:

Concentration = 12M/ml

Motility = 91%

TMC Calculation: 12 x 0.91= 10.92

Insemination Volume: 75/10.92 = 6.87µl
