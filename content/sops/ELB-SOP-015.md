## 1. Purpose

To define how biohazardous and sharps waste generated in the Embryology and Andrology Laboratory is segregated, contained, stored and removed, so that staff, patients and the environment are protected from infectious material and sharps injury. This SOP also defines how it interacts with — and never overrides — the disposition of patient gametes and embryos, which are governed by consent.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all biological waste arising in the laboratory: semen and processed sperm waste, follicular fluid, used culture media and dishes that contacted biological material, blood-contaminated items, and sharps (needles, pipettes, slides). Disposition of patients' own gametes and embryos is **out of scope here** and is governed by consent and ELB-POL-009 / disposition records — embryos and oocytes are never discarded as routine waste. Applies to autologous workflows only; donor and surrogacy work is not performed.

> **CRITICAL — material under consent is not waste.** A patient's gametes or embryos are disposed of only on the authority of a recorded disposition election (ELB-POL-009, ELB-LOG-017), witnessed (ELB-SOP-002), never via this waste stream.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **All staff** — segregate waste correctly at the point of generation and use sharps containers as specified.
- **Laboratory Lead** — maintains containers, the collection contract and the storage area.
- **Quality Manager** — audits segregation and records and reviews any incident.
- **Medical Director** — owns exposure-control policy (ELB-POL-001).

## 4. Definitions & abbreviations

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Biohazardous waste | Waste contaminated with potentially infectious biological material |
| Sharps | Items capable of penetrating skin (needles, glass, pipette tips) |
| Segregation | Separating waste streams at the point of generation |
| PPE | Personal protective equipment |

## 5. Equipment, materials & media

- Colour-coded/biohazard-labelled bags and rigid biohazard containers.
- Puncture-resistant sharps containers with fill lines.
- PPE (non-latex gloves, ELB-POL-003; eye protection as needed).
- Disinfectant for surfaces/spills (embryo-safe and GHS-labelled, ELB-SOP-014).
- Spill kit for biological spills.

> **IFU / labelling cross-reference.** Containers are used per the supplier specification and the local waste contract; disinfectants are GHS-labelled and used per IFU. Sharps containers are not filled above the marked line.

## 6. Pre-procedure checks

1. Confirm biohazard bags, containers and sharps bins are in place, labelled and below fill limits.
2. Confirm a spill kit and disinfectant are available.
3. Confirm PPE is worn before handling waste.

## 7. Procedure

1. Segregate at source: biological waste into biohazard containers; sharps directly into sharps bins — never re-cap or transfer sharps by hand.
2. Keep chemical waste separate from biohazardous waste (ELB-SOP-014); do not combine incompatible streams.
3. Do not overfill; seal and replace containers at the fill line.
4. Disinfect surfaces and any spill per the SDS/IFU; for spills, contain, disinfect, then clear wearing PPE.
5. Move sealed waste to the designated interim storage; record removal by the licensed contractor.
6. Decontaminate reusable items destined for sterilisation separately (ELB-SOP-011, ELB-SOP-012).
7. Perform hand hygiene after handling waste and removing gloves.

## 8. Critical control points & witnessing

Waste handling does not create gamete-identity events and so carries no per-step gamete witnessing. The one identity-critical interface is confirming that nothing under consent has entered the waste stream.

> **CRITICAL CONTROL POINT.** Before sealing any container that held dishes/tubes from a case, confirm no labelled patient gamete or embryo vessel is present. Disposition of patient material is witnessed and recorded separately (ELB-SOP-002, ELB-LOG-017).

## 9. Acceptance / rejection criteria

Waste handling is compliant when streams are segregated, containers are labelled and within fill limits, sharps are never handled loose, and no consented material has entered the waste stream. Any breach is a reject/incident condition.

## 10. Deviations

Sharps injuries, spills, mis-segregation and any exposure are handled under the exposure-control policy (ELB-POL-001) and the deviation/CAPA policy (ELB-POL-006), and recorded in the deviation log (ELB-LOG-023). Personal exposures follow ELB-POL-001 immediately.

## 11. Records generated

- Waste collection/removal records (licensed contractor).
- Exposure/incident entries — per ELB-POL-001 and **ELB-LOG-023**.
- Disposition of patient material (separate stream) — **ELB-LOG-017**.

## 12. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

Container types, segregation scheme and interim-storage limits 🔧 and the disinfectant/contact times 🔧 are recorded in **Appendix A** and the local waste contract. Labelling aligns with **GHS**; safe-handling and facility control align with ISO 15189. (OSHA is not cited.)

## 13. References

- ISO 15189 — requirements for quality and competence in medical laboratories (safety, facilities).
- GHS — labelling of chemical-contaminated and hazardous waste containers.

## 14. Teaching point

The one mistake this SOP exists to prevent is treating consented material as rubbish. Sharps and biohazard discipline protect the staff; the segregation check protects the patient. A dish from a case may look like waste, but until you have confirmed it holds no labelled gamete or embryo, it is not — and embryo disposition is always a witnessed, consent-driven act, never a bin.
