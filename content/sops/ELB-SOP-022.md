## 1. Purpose

To define the standardised method for preparing a semen sample for intrauterine insemination (IUI) — recovering a motile, washed, seminal-plasma-free fraction in a small volume suitable for direct uterine instillation — and for releasing it safely and traceably to the treating clinician.

## 2. Scope

Applies to preparation of fresh or thawed (autologous) sperm from the **male partner of the patient couple** for IUI. Preparation for IVF/ICSI is covered in ELB-SOP-019; thawing of frozen samples in ELB-SOP-020; surgical sperm in ELB-SOP-021. Donor insemination is out of scope and is not performed.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Performing embryologist / andrology technologist** — trained and signed off (ELB-SOP-005); prepares the sample, performs the witnessing checkpoints and records.
- **Witness (second embryologist or RI Witness electronic system)** — confirms patient identity and label match at each transfer (ELB-SOP-002).
- **Treating clinician** — confirms patient identity at handover and performs the insemination.
- **Senior embryologist / Laboratory Lead** — reviews borderline yields and any deviation.

## 4. Definitions & abbreviations

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| IUI | Intrauterine insemination — instillation of prepared sperm into the uterine cavity |
| TMSC | Total motile sperm count in the final preparation |
| DGC | Density-gradient centrifugation |
| Final volume | The small instillation volume of medium into which the motile fraction is resuspended |
| EW | Electronic witnessing (RI Witness) |

## 5. Equipment, materials & media

- Class II laminar flow hood (ELB-SOP-044); centrifuge; heated surfaces at the working temperature (**Appendix A**).
- Density-gradient and sperm-washing media validated for IUI use (no toxic additives), within shelf life, lots recorded on **ELB-LOG-003**.
- Sterile conical tubes, pipettes; sterile IUI catheter/tube for handover; counting chamber; microscope with heated stage; RI Witness reader.

> **IFU cross-reference.** Use only media and labware confirmed suitable for intrauterine use per the manufacturer Instructions For Use. Do not introduce gradient medium into the final instillation fraction — wash thoroughly per IFU.

## 6. Pre-procedure checks

1. Confirm a current order and consent for IUI, the planned insemination time, and coordination with the clinical team.
2. Confirm the sample is received, identified and liquefied (ELB-SOP-018) or correctly thawed (ELB-SOP-020).
3. Confirm the **baseline assessment** (volume, concentration, motility — per **WHO** manual) on **ELB-LOG-002**.
4. Confirm media are at working temperature and labelled to the patient.

> **CRITICAL — identity before processing.** Verify the sample label against the patient and worklist and complete an EW identity match before opening the tube (ELB-SOP-016, ELB-SOP-002). Only one patient's sample is open at the workstation.

## 7. Procedure

1. Confirm sample identity; perform the **EW identity match** (Section 8, checkpoint W1).
2. Prepare the motile fraction by **density-gradient centrifugation** (default) or swim-up per ELB-SOP-019, selecting by sample quality (Appendix A).
3. Wash the recovered fraction thoroughly to remove all gradient medium and seminal plasma — prostaglandins and debris must not be instilled — per IFU.
4. Resuspend the motile pellet in the small defined **final volume** of IUI-suitable medium (Appendix A / IFU).
5. Assess the final preparation: concentration, progressive motility, volume; calculate **TMSC**.
6. Record method, media lots, settings, final volume and **TMSC** on **ELB-LOG-003**.
7. Load the preparation into the sterile IUI device labelled to the patient; keep at working temperature.
8. Release to the clinician only after a documented identity handover and **EW match** (checkpoint W2; ELB-SOP-017).

## 8. Critical control points & witnessing

Witnessing is mandatory at processing and at handover (ELB-SOP-002). RI Witness performs the electronic match; a manual second-person check is the fallback if EW is unavailable.

| Checkpoint | What is verified | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **W1** | Sample identity vs patient vs worklist before processing | EW match (RI Witness) + label read |
| **W2** | Prepared IUI sample correctly labelled and handed to the clinician for the right patient | EW match (ELB-SOP-017) |

> **CRITICAL CONTROL POINT.** A failed or absent witness match halts the procedure or the handover. Resolve identity and re-witness before continuing. The clinician must not inseminate until the laboratory handover is witnessed. Log any EW downtime and the manual fallback used.

## 9. Acceptance / rejection criteria

- A preparation is **acceptable for IUI** when the final **TMSC**, progressive motility and volume meet the locally defined thresholds (🔧 **Appendix A**) and the fraction is free of seminal plasma and gradient medium.
- A **low yield** below the IUI threshold is escalated to the senior embryologist and clinician; consider repeating, or converting the cycle plan (e.g. to IVF/ICSI). Record the decision.
- A preparation suspected of carry-over contamination or wrong-medium error is rejected and remade.

## 10. Deviations

Any departure — low TMSC, incomplete washing, centrifuge fault, EW downtime, or a handover identity discrepancy — is managed under the deviation and CAPA policy (ELB-POL-006) and recorded in the deviation log (ELB-LOG-023).

## 11. Records generated

- Semen analysis worksheet (baseline) — **ELB-LOG-002**.
- Sperm preparation worksheet — **ELB-LOG-003** (method, lots, final volume, **TMSC**).
- Custody / handover entry — **ELB-SOP-017**.
- Any deviation entry — **ELB-LOG-023**.

## 12. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

Values are tabulated in **Appendix A (ELB-APP-A)**. Semen reference values follow the current **WHO** manual; preparation methods are **guideline-anchored** (ESHRE 2015; ASRM 2022). Centrifuge settings and final-volume guidance follow the media IFU. The minimum acceptable post-preparation **TMSC / motility for IUI** is **locally defined** (flagged 🔧, requiring Medical Director sign-off).

## 14. Teaching point

IUI is the one preparation that goes directly into a patient, so what you *remove* matters as much as what you keep: seminal plasma and gradient medium left in the final fraction can cause cramping or harm, and must be washed out completely. The whole value of IUI rests on delivering a clean, concentrated, motile fraction in a small volume — and on a witnessed handover, because here the prepared sample leaves the laboratory's control and enters the patient within minutes.
