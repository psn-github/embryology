#### Purpose

The primary objective of this SOP is to outline the biopsy procedure and associated techniques such as tubing and vitrification. Blastocyst biopsy for PGT analysis is the process whereby trophectoderm cells are removed from the blastocyst and subject to genetic analysis. The technique is used to provide a source of DNA for preimplantation genetic testing of embryos created in the IVF laboratory.

There are different types of genetic testing and the type used is determined by the genetic target:

The purpose of PGT-A is to define the presence or absence of chromosomes or segmental aneuploidies. Studies have shown that testing embryos to determine chromosome complement can improve implantation and pregnancy outcomes (depending on age group), decrease the time to achieve pregnancy and reduce the risk of miscarriage but this remains to be conclusively proven.

The purpose of PGT-SR is screening embryos for unbalanced chromosome rearrangements (segmental gains and/or losses) resulting from mis- segregation of parental chromosomal rearrangements (translocations, inversions, and other complex structural rearrangements).

The purpose of PGT-M is to identify embryos with a particular haplotype, based upon genetic testing reports and an oral pedigree. This technique is used to avoid the inheritance of single gene disorders whereby there is significant risk of a person having/developing a serious physical or mental illness/medical condition. No other mutations or genetic variants are tested. It can be implemented in conjunction with PGT-A.

Only tests authorised by the HFEA may be used to genetically test embryos. In addition, it is not permitted to use information derived from genetic tests to select embryos of a particular sex for social reasons.  <!-- TODO(§8 hfea): HFEA / UK licence-condition reference — re-base to Kuwait MoH equivalent or remove (§8). #### Definitions

PGT Pre-implantation genetic testing

PGT-A Pre-implantation genetic testing for aneuploidy. The detection is based on NGS.

PGT-SR Pre-implantation genetic testing for structural rearrangements. The detection is based on NGS. Karyomapping may be utilised in certain situations.

PGT-M Pre-implantation genetic testing for monogenic disease. The detection is based on Karyomapping (for single gene disorder) and NGS for ploidy. Where possible (depending on mutation for specific monogenic condition) Sanger sequencing may be performed.

PGTai 2.0+ This platform combines copy number variation data with SNP analysis allowing detection of all forms of ploidy. Therefore, suspected 1PN embryos could be re-tested. It requires parental cheek swabs.

NGS Next Generation Sequencing. Based on whole genome amplification and massive parallel sequencing. NGS can directly detect chromosomal gains/deletions or partial gains/deletions. Its limitations are described below.

STR Short tandem repeat. A repeated pattern of several nucleotides which are directly adjacent to each other.

SNP Single nucleotide polymorphism. A genomic variant at a single base position within DNA.

Karyomapping Array based platform that can detect copy number changes, SNPs and SNPs charts. It works on a linkage basis and studies the DNA of multiple family members establishing which copy (or combination of copies) of a gene should be excluded (as old style PGD but using SNPs rather than STRs). It is not direct test of a mutation or deletion.

Tubing Transferring biopsied cells to a PCR tube aseptically.

Euploid Samples reported as 'Euploid' have been determined to contain the expected chromosome complement within the limitations of NGS.

Aneuploid Samples reported as 'Aneuploid' have been determined to contain a chromosome constitution that differs from that expected for a healthy cell.

Trisomy An extra copy of any chromosome, i.e. Trisomy 21

Monosomy The loss of one copy of any chromosome, i.e. Monosomy X

Polyploidy Extra copy(ies) of the entire chromosome complement.

Segmental Duplication or deletion of a specific segment of, but not the whole, chromosome.

#### 5.2 Equipment and consumables checks

Prior to the start of the procedure the equipment to be used must be checked to ensure it is operating optimally:

-   Gloves must be used during hood set up and tubing to reduce risk of contamination.
-   The flow-hood must be checked to ensure the flow is switched on and the fan must be set to high speed.
    -   All unnecessary dishes/pipettes and tubes must be removed from the hood and surfaces cleaned thoroughly with Oosafe +/- water.
    -   Ensure the RI heated stage is switched on and at the correct set point.
    -   Ensure the biopsy heated stage on the microscope is switched on and at the correct set point.
    -   Ensure laser is tested and align if necessary. Calibrate at 6 – 12 months.
    -   Ensure that Cooper’s PCR rack is labelled with patient details.
    -   Ensure that Cooper’s PCR rack contains the relevant PCR tubes labelled with Cooper’s QR code sticker and the hand-written embryo number is clear.

#### 5.3 Blastocysts suitable for trophectoderm biopsy:

#### All blastocysts grade C or above are suitable for biopsy. Embryos with lower score can be biopsied at embryologist’s discretion following full discussion with the patient.

#### 5.4 Considerations and required paperwork before starting treatment

Each laboratory is responsible for checking upcoming biopsy cycles using IDEAS and preferably in conjunction with a PGT-nurse/clinical co-ordinator. Relevant progress notes should be created on the patient file.

A Test Requisition Form must be completed, signed by a clinician and sent to [globalsupport@coopergenomics.com](mailto:globalsupport@coopergenomics.com). This must be completed for the acceptance of the samples and courier arrangement.

For PGT-A requisitions on the PGTaiSM 2.0 platform, ensure that “PGT-A via PGTaiSM 2.0 platform without mosaic reporting” is selected in the “test Information” section.

All PGT cycles should use time-lapse incubation where possible for easier laboratory organisation on the day of the biopsy where possible. TBR cases may be either traditional or time-lapse incubated.

##### On Day 0:

IVF or ICSI can be routinely performed for PGT-A cases using the PGTai 2.0 platform.

IVF can be performed for female translocation PGT-SR cases.

ICSI must be performed for PGT-M and male PGT-SR cases.

CooperGenomics do accept some cases of IVF in PGT-M but these MUST be agreed prior to cycle start on a case-by-case basis.

##### On Day 1:

Corona cells must be completely removed from the zona pellucida during denudation/fertilization check to avoid DNA cross contamination.

##### On Day 3-7:

Assisted hatching can be performed on day 3, 4, 5, 6 or 7 as per laboratory preference.

#### 5.5 Witnessing

For full details of PGT-A witnessing requirements please refer to Oxford Medical Kuwait Witnessing procedure (TFP1-SOP-EMB-0028). In summary the following witness steps are required during the biopsy and vitrification:

-   Transfer of embryo from ES+GTL Dish to biopsy dish – three patient identifiers confirmed alongside RI witness. Second witness confirms correct embryo moved to biopsy dish.
-   Transfer of biopsy to correct microcentrifuge tube – second witness confirms embryo number on biopsy dish with that on microcentrifuge tube.
-   Transfer of embryo to vitrification dish - three patient identifiers confirmed alongside RI witness.
-   Rapid I barcode label checked against vitrification dish – three patient identifiers (including embryo number) confirmed alongside RI and a second witness.
-   At the end of the procedure, Rapid I barcode label(s) checked against Visitube / cassette label. Visitube / cassette placed in correct storage location. confirmed alongside second witness.

**5.7 Preparation of Biopsy Micromanipulator**

Check that the microscope is in the correct focal plane for a biopsy dish, ensure all controls are operating smoothly and are in mid-range of their full movement potential.

Insert the biopsy and holding pipettes into the pipette holder and ensure a tight seal. Ensure that the angle of the pipette holders matches the angle of the needles.

At x40 both biopsy and holding pipettes should be in focus, have a central position and be approximately parallel to the base of the dish. Check the pipettes for any sign of damage and replace if necessary.

Raise both pipettes so that the biopsy dish can be placed on the heated stage with ease.

#### 5.8 Hood Set Up

#### Clearly label each biopsy dish with the embryo number, patient name, DOB and unique identifier before placing the dishes in a non- gassed incubator for a minimum of one hour.

Prepare a 200\*-290µl pre-pulled pipette for blastocyst manipulation during the biopsy and use a 130-145µl flexi pipette for the trophectoderm samples as necessary or wash your flexi pipette thoroughly before taking biopsied cells.

\* if using a 200µl to move the blast to its biopsy dish and rinse embryo - the blast may start collapse making the biopsy easier.

The bag containing the PCR tubes must be opened inside the flow-hood to maintain sterility. One PCR tube should be prepared for each embryo intended for biopsy. These must be labelled, adopting an aseptic technique, with a QR sticker (supplied in the biopsy kit) and the embryo number on the lid.

**5.9 Laser Alignment, Calibration and Troubleshooting**

The laser should be aligned, and the size of the pulse calibrated 6-12 months. Several factors can modify these parameters, but the most common are IT updates or aging lasers. Calibrate laser according to the manufacturer’s guidelines. If calibration is not possible, risk assess and discuss with Head of Department.

#### 5.10 Blastocyst Biopsy

Wash the first blastocyst to be biopsied and place it in the biopsy dish labelled with the same embryo number.

Transfer the biopsy dish to the micromanipulator heated stage ensuring the entire surface area of the dish is on the heated stage.

Visualise the drops within the dish and adjust the focus as necessary. Prime the biopsy and holding pipettes and ensure control of media/no travel. Focus on the edge of the zona pellucida and use gently suction to secure the holding pipette close to (but not directly adjacent to) the inner cell mass (ICM).

For blastocysts that have not been assisted hatched, focus the laser on the edge of the zona pellucida and fire a succession of small holes (5-20µm) to penetrate through the zona. TE cell removal is often easier if the blastocyst has started to collapse, and TE cells have detached from the zona. Take at least 5-8 trophectoderm cells gently (to avoid cell lysis) into the biopsy pipette and identify the cell junctions in between the biopsy pipette and the blastocyst. Aim to fire no more than three shots to release tension between the trophectoderm (TE) cells (0.2-0.3ms for Saturn laser and 1.3ms for Octax Laser). More shots may be required for the non-flick method of biopsy but must be minimized.

Continue until the TE cells have detached from the blastocyst, taking care to control the TE cells travelling up the biopsy pipette. Ensure that the same cell junction is not lasered more than once.

Practitioners may use the ‘flick’ technique to detach the cells by holding the trophectoderm cells with the biopsy pipette, detaching the blastocyst from the holding pipette and striking the biopsy needle against the holding pipette at the desired point of detachment.

Ensure the TE cells are completely detached from the blastocyst and gently release the blastocyst and biopsied cells spacing them apart within the drop.

Aspirate the biopsy pipette in a washing drop before proceeding with the next biopsy. Blastocysts can be returned to GTL/G2 if tubing/vitrification after all biopsies are completed.

Use pre-prepared vitrification dishes in accordance with Oxford Medical Kuwait Blastocyst Vitrification – Vitrolife (TFP1-SOP-EMB-0016) procedure to vitrify each embryo post-biopsy/tubing.

-   -   Each straw must only contain one biopsied blastocyst.
    -   Straws must be labelled/witnessed with the patient’s full name, unit number, freeze date and **unique embryo number**.

#### 5.11 Tubing

Wearing gloves and with the aid of a pipettor, prepare the dishes for tubing following the layout described earlier. DO NOT COVER WITH OIL. Maintain at room temperature, do not place on heated stage.

The corresponding PCR microtube must be witnessed before the TE cells are transferred from the biopsy dish. Once witnessed, rinse the 135-145µl denuding pipette in the top drop (30µl droplet) at least five times.

Transfer the biopsied TE cells from the biopsy dish to the second wash drop (first 15µl drop). Empty the pipette and rinse the cells carefully. It is imperative to pay close attention to the cells to keep track of them during the washing process. Priming from the subsequent drop, wash the cells through drops 2-4.

Transfer the TE cells to the microtube whilst visualising the sample under the stereo microscope aiming for a total of 2.5-3µl of media. Close the tube, spin it in a micro- centrifuge if required and place the tube in a chilled rack following the witnessing process for PGT in the Oxford Medical Kuwait witnessing SOP. Remaining gloved, put the labelled rack inside the sealed plastic bag provided and return the samples to the freezer.

N.B. If a separate ice rack has been used during tubing, a witness should be asked to manually witness this step. The embryologist moving the samples must wear gloves. The embryo number on the tube are checked against the biopsy paperwork. The number of tubes moved is also checked against the paperwork. This process should be performed as quickly as possible, avoiding touching the bottom of the tubes where the samples are located.

Clear the flow-hood and micromanipulator of any used consumables. Use Oosafe to clean the surfaces. Complete the relevant embryology notes, witnessing records and IDEAS database.

#### 5.12 Paperwork, Packaging and Transportation

Complete all sections of the Cooper Genomics (CG) Biopsy Worksheet sheet and place a copy inside the embryo biopsy kit supplied by CG or email directly to globalsupport@coopergenomics.com. It is imperative that numbering on the microtubes is consistent with the numbering on the biopsy paperwork. Any discrepancies between the numbering may result in sample rejection by CG.

Requesting a courier via CG when sending the Biopsy Worksheet. Immediately prior to collection, place ice packs inside a polystyrene box with the PCR rack surrounded above and below. Use appropriate packaging to ensure the rack is secure. If clinic practice, biopsy paperwork can be placed in a sealed bag and put inside the polystyrene box. Close the polystyrene box and secure with tape. Attach the CG address label. Confirm that the box has been collected before leaving the clinic. Update IDEAs as appropriate and ensure CG confirm the sample receipt within 24 hours.

**5.13 Blastocyst Biopsy for Frozen Embryos (Thaw-Biopsy-Refreeze (TBR))**

A patient may decide to perform PGT on zygotes, embryos or blastocysts that have already been frozen during a previous treatment cycle including those that were previously biopsied with no result. Zygotes and embryos should be cultured to the blastocyst stage.

Patients must be fully informed on the risks (described below) and they must complete a “thaw biopsy refreeze” consent form.

-   Blastocyst thawing survival is >95%
-   Potential detrimental effect of removing more TE cells from the blastocyst in re-biopsy cases.
-   Re-vitrifying a blastocyst after the biopsy process may impact survival/chance of pregnancy.
-   Blastocyst survival after a second thaw is estimated to be similar than first thaw.
-   Not all embryos may be suitable to be retested due to its morphological quality pre or post warm.

Warming should be performed in line with the warming SOP and embryos cultured post thaw in a GTL ES+ dish (for ease of identifying when the optimal time for biopsying the blastocyst is). The embryo number should be maintained wherever possible or a new, fully documented identifier given. Embryos with a previous “no result” do not need assisted hatching. Thaw-Biopsy-Re-Vitrified blastocyst tubes and notes should be labelled with V-prefix for example “V-2”.

**5.14 Whole Embryo Tubing for Arrested Embryos**

In some PGT-M cases where there is a lack of reference DNA from affected parents, the use of arrested embryos may be necessary (usually for autosomal dominant cases). Arrested embryos are tubed and sent to the genetics lab with the biopsy samples. The couple may need further cycles before the results can be reported if there are insufficient arrested embryos to test. Embryos should be zoneless/well breached.

#### 5.15 PGT Results - New Oxford Medical Kuwait Reporting Thresholds

### PGT-SR Standard Reporting

Normal or Balanced: Samples contain the expected chromosome complement. **Suitable for transfer.**

Unbalanced: Samples reported as ‘Unbalanced’ have been found to contain a gain or loss of DNA for the whole or part of the chromosomes involved in the tested structural chromosome rearrangement. **NOT advised for transfer.**

No Result: The laboratory is unable to obtain a result for a particular sample. **The chromosomal status should be considered unknown.**

-   **Failed amplification** – no DNA was detected in the tube
-   **Insufficient quality of DNA** – DNA present but not sufficient quality/quantity to obtain result.

### PGT-M Standard Reporting

Unaffected: An embryo that is found to have inherited normal copy(ies). **Suitable for transfer.**

Affected: An embryo that is found to have inherited mutant copy(ies) of the gene. **NOT advised for transfer.**

Carrier: An embryo that is found to have inherited one mutant copy of the gene and one normal copy of the gene. **Transfer possible following discussion.**

Mutant Allele Not Detected:

Testing of the sample did not detect the mutant copy of the gene and/or chromosomal segment from the carrier parent. **Suitable for transfer.**

No Result: The laboratory is unable to obtain a result for a particular sample. **The chromosomal status should be considered unknown.**

-   **Failed amplification** – no DNA was detected in the tube
-   **Insufficient quality of DNA** – DNA present but not sufficient quality/quantity to obtain result.

### PGT-SR & PGT-M Reporting with PGT-A

PGT-A can be performed in conjunction with PGT-SR and PGT-M testing. In line with the revised Oxford Medical Kuwait reporting strategy, mosaic reporting WILL NOT be reported in these cases – see description below.

### PGT-A Reporting

##### **Default:** The Oxford Medical Kuwait reporting strategy for PGT-A is based on whole chromosome, segmental or ‘special chromosome’ changes. Each embryo will yield either a Euploid or Aneuploid result based on the euploid:aneuploid thresholds below.

##### Whole Chromosome ≤60% = Euploid >60% = Aneuploid

Segmental Changes ≤80% = Euploid >80% = Aneuploid

Special Whole Chromosomes 13, 18, 21 ≤20% = Euploid >20% = Aneuploid

Euploid: accepted chromosome complement. **Suitable for transfer.**

Aneuploid: chromosome constitution differs from the expected. **NOT advised for transfer.**

Mosaic Reporting Option: Referring clinicians can specifically request mosaic reporting for PGT-A cases if they feel it is clinically relevant. Results will then be reported as described below:

###### _PGT-A, if Mosaic Reporting Requested_

Euploid: expected chromosome complement in >80% of the cells. **Suitable for transfer.**

Aneuploid: chromosome constitution differs from the expected. **NOT advised for transfer.** Specific aneuploidies identified may include:

-   Trisomy: an extra copy of any chromosome
-   Monosomy: loss of one copy of any chromosome
-   Polyploidy: extra copy of the entire chromosome complement
-   Segmental: duplication or deletion of a specific segment of a chromosome.

Mosaic: A significant proportion of the cells sampled are found to have a chromosome abnormality:

-   Low Level Mosaic: Low level mosaic samples (20-40% mosaic) **Transfer possible following discussion with clinician/counsellor/genetic counsellor.**
-   High Level Mosaic: High level mosaic samples (>40-80% mosaic) **NOT advised for transfer.**

Complex Abnormal: Samples contain three or more aneuploidies. **NOT advised for transfer.**

No Result: The laboratory is unable to obtain a result for a particular sample. **The chromosomal status should be considered unknown.**

-   **Failed amplification** – no DNA was detected in the tube
-   **Insufficient quality of DNA** – DNA present but not sufficient quality/quantity to obtain result.

#### 5.16 Reporting Results

Results should be communicated as per local pathway.

**Original results must be used as the primary source of information at embryo warm.**

Patients should be booked for a follow-up consultation to either plan a Frozen Embryo Transfer cycle (if transferable embryos available) or to discuss future treatment plans if no result, possible transfer or no transferable embryos are available. Patients can be offered further counselling.

#### 5.17 Technical limitations of PGT

PGT-A via NGS, has an accuracy rate of >97% and cannot guarantee a chromosomally normal offspring. It cannot detect rearrangements <5MB in size.

PGT-SR via NGS testing is reported to have an accuracy rate >95% but cannot guarantee a chromosomally normal offspring. It cannot distinguish a balanced translocation from a normal chromosomal compliment.

PGT-M testing is reported to have an accuracy rate >95% but cannot guarantee a chromosomally normal offspring.

PGT-A, SR or M does not replace the recommendation for conventional pre-natal testing where it would be clinically indicated. Genetic counselling is also strongly recommended.

#### 5.18 Counselling

Patients should be advised of the limitations of the techniques used for PGT. They should be fully aware that any genetic test based on sampling one or a small number of cells biopsied from preimplantation embryos cannot be 100% accurate for a combination of technical and biological factors.

Patient information and consent forms for PGT should include all potential test results and any potential risks in the event of transfer and implantation of a tested embryo.

Cooper Genomics has a genetic counseling service. To refer a patient to them, provide the patient’s contact details (email and contact number) to Cooper Genomics global support and they will contact the patient. Genetic counselling from Cooper Genomics is included in the Oxford Medical Kuwait PGT-SR and PGT-M pricing.

1.  **Standards/Key Performance/Quality Indicators**

The following KPIs are expected from blastocyst biopsy:

-   -   -   -   The time length of the biopsy should be less than 5 minutes per blastocyst
            -   The biopsy should consist of 5 to 8 cells.
            -   Blastocyst damage ≤5%
            -   Failed amplification rate ≤5%
            -   Implantation rate ≥45% per embryo transferred
            -   Miscarriage rate ≤15% per pregnancy

If these results fall below KPI, review the biopsy and/or tubing technique with an experienced practitioner. Discuss with clinical team to confirm that other parameters remain the same.
