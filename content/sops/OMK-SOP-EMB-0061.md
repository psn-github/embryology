## 1. Purpose

To define how temperature is monitored across all temperature-critical equipment and surfaces in the Embryology Laboratory — from cryogenic storage to incubators, heated stages, warming blocks, fridges and freezers — so that gametes and embryos are held within their defined ranges and any excursion is detected, alarmed and acted upon before it harms stored or cultured material.

## 2. Scope

Applies to monitoring of: cryogenic storage tank temperatures (with OMK-SOP-EMB-0045), CO₂ incubators (with OMK-SOP-EMB-0062), heated stages, warming blocks and tube warmers, media/reagent refrigerators and freezers, and ambient laboratory conditions where specified. It covers continuous monitoring, scheduled manual checks, alarm response and the calibration linkage. Thermometer calibration itself is in OMK-SOP-EMB-0064. The scope is autologous material only.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Embryologist on duty** — performs and records scheduled manual checks and responds to local alarms.
- **Designated equipment custodian** — maintains the continuous-monitoring system, the alarm contact list and the calibration schedule (OMK-SOP-EMB-0064).
- **Laboratory Lead** — sets the acceptable ranges and alarm thresholds (Appendix A) and the out-of-hours escalation.
- **Quality Manager** — reviews monitoring records, excursions and trends as part of equipment QC (OMK-SOP-EMB-0060).

## 4. Definitions & abbreviations

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Continuous monitoring | Automated logging of temperature with local and remote alarms |
| Excursion | A reading outside the defined acceptable range for that item |
| Reference thermometer | A calibrated device used to verify the monitoring sensor (OMK-SOP-EMB-0064) |
| In-drop / in-situ verification | Confirming the true temperature at the point material sits, not just the display |
| Alarm threshold | The value at which an alert is raised, set inside the harm boundary |

## 5. Equipment, materials & media

- Continuous temperature-monitoring system with calibrated probes on critical equipment, data logging and local + remote/out-of-hours alarms.
- Calibrated reference thermometer(s) traceable to standard, used for verification (OMK-SOP-EMB-0064).
- Independent local thermometers/displays on incubators, heated stages, warming blocks, fridges and freezers.
- LN₂ tank temperature sensors (operated under OMK-SOP-EMB-0045).

> **IFU / calibration cross-reference.** Monitoring sensors and reference thermometers are used per their IFU and verified against a traceable standard on the schedule in Appendix A (OMK-SOP-EMB-0064). A display is trusted only after the sensor behind it has been verified.

## 6. Pre-procedure checks

1. Confirm the continuous-monitoring system is logging, the clock is correct and no probe is in fault.
2. Confirm alarms are armed and the out-of-hours contact list is current.
3. Confirm the reference thermometer is within its calibration date before any verification (OMK-SOP-EMB-0064).
4. Confirm each critical item's acceptable range and alarm thresholds are the current values from Appendix A.

> **CRITICAL — verify where the cells live.** For culture and warming, the value that matters is the temperature in the drop and on the working surface, not the equipment display. In-situ verification is performed on the schedule in Appendix A and after any service or relocation.

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Continuous monitoring
1. Maintain a calibrated probe on each critical item; ensure data is logged continuously and retained.
2. Confirm daily that the system is logging and that no item sits silently outside its range.

### 7.2 Scheduled manual checks
1. At the defined frequency 🔧 (Appendix A), read and record each item's temperature against its acceptable range.
2. Record cryogenic tank temperatures with the LN₂ monitoring entries (OMK-LOG-EMB-0064, OMK-SOP-EMB-0045).
3. Where required, perform in-drop/in-situ verification with the reference thermometer and record the offset against the display.

### 7.3 Verification against reference
1. On the schedule in Appendix A, compare each monitoring sensor with the calibrated reference thermometer.
2. If the difference exceeds the allowed tolerance 🔧 (Appendix A), flag the sensor for recalibration or replacement (OMK-SOP-EMB-0064) and treat affected readings with caution.

### 7.4 Alarm response
1. On an excursion or alarm, confirm it is real (cross-check with an independent thermometer), then protect the material: move it to a verified in-range unit if the excursion threatens it.
2. Identify and correct the cause; record the excursion, its duration, the readings and the action as a deviation (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005).
3. Assess and record the impact on any affected gametes/embryos and escalate where indicated (OMK-POL-EMB-0004).

## 8. Critical control points & witnessing

Temperature monitoring is an equipment control rather than a gamete-handling step, so routine witnessing does not apply; however, any movement of material to a backup unit during an excursion is witnessed (OMK-SOP-EMB-0002) and recorded.

| Checkpoint | What is verified | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** | Continuous logging active and alarms armed for all critical items | Daily system check |
| **C2** | Sensor agreement with the calibrated reference within tolerance | Scheduled verification (OMK-SOP-EMB-0064) |
| **C3** | Material relocated during an excursion matches its records | Witness + record |

> **CRITICAL CONTROL POINT.** An alarm is acted on, not silenced. Acknowledging an alarm without confirming the reading and protecting the material is itself a deviation. Alarm thresholds are set inside the harm boundary so that there is time to respond before damage occurs.

## 9. Acceptance / rejection criteria

- A reading is **acceptable** when it is within the item's defined range 🔧 (Appendix A); incubator targets and cryogenic ranges are guideline/tank-anchored.
- A sensor **passes verification** when its difference from the reference thermometer is within the allowed tolerance 🔧; otherwise it is recalibrated or replaced (OMK-SOP-EMB-0064).
- Any sustained excursion beyond the threshold requires impact assessment of the affected material before it is used.

## 10. Deviations

An excursion, a failed verification, a probe fault, a missed alarm or a logging gap is managed under the deviation and CAPA policy (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005) and recorded in the deviation log (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005). Any excursion that may have harmed stored or cultured material is a reportable adverse event (OMK-POL-EMB-0004).

## 11. Records generated

- Continuous-monitoring data and excursion records (system log) — retained per OMK-SOP-EMB-0004.
- Manual temperature-check records, including in-situ verification offsets.
- LN₂ tank temperature entries — **OMK-LOG-EMB-0064** (OMK-SOP-EMB-0045).
- Any deviation / excursion entry — **OMK-SOP-EMB-0005**.

## 12. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

Culture temperature (37.0 °C) and pH-linked targets are **guideline-anchored** (ESHRE 2015); cryogenic storage range is tank/phase-anchored (OMK-SOP-EMB-0045). Values tabulated in **Appendix A (OMK-APP-EMB-A)** include: the manual-check frequency for each item class 🔧, the alarm thresholds for incubators, fridges/freezers and tanks 🔧, the sensor-to-reference verification tolerance 🔧, and the in-situ verification schedule 🔧. Each locally defined value is flagged 🔧 in Appendix A and requires Medical Director sign-off.

## 14. Teaching point

Temperature is the variable embryos feel most and complain about least — by the time damage is visible it is already done. The system therefore works backwards from harm: set the alarm inside the boundary so there is time to act, trust the in-drop reading over the glowing display, and verify the sensor against a calibrated reference so that the number you are trusting is true. A monitoring record is only as good as the day you last proved the probe was honest.
