#### Purpose

Regulatory bodies and Oxford Medical Kuwait require that witnessing protocols must be in place to ensure that gametes and embryos can be identified at all stages of the laboratory and treatment process to prevent any mismatches between patients’ gametes/embryos. To ensure that witnessing procedures within TFP are conducted in line with the requirements of the HFE Act (or equivalent) and to ensure that gametes and embryos can be identified, traced, and correctly matched as they pass through the culture system.

#### Scope

Ensuring that patients and their samples are correctly identified and witnessed at each stage of the treatment process requires a multidisciplinary approach. This SOP is therefore applicable to members of the clinical, nursing and laboratory teams trained to undertake witnessing procedures.

Gamete/embryo donation and surrogacy are out of scope (prohibited in Kuwait).

#### Procedure

##### 5.1 General Guidance

Witnessing must only be carried out by suitably trained staff whose full name, job title and signature is recorded in the clinic’s staff signatory log (TFP1-FRM-EMB-0027 or equivalent).

Each staff member must have their own unique RI login and/or fob.

Laboratory, nursing, clinical or administrative staff are all suitable to witness; subject to appropriate training (Oxford Medical Kuwait Witnessing Training, TFP1-SCT-HR-0019). Having a multi-disciplinary approach to witnessing helps to ensure witnessing is as efficient as possible. Patient’s may also cross-check their own identifying information, for example at oocyte retrieval or semen sample collection.

All staff witnessing, whether alongside a second member of staff or using RI Witness, must perform ‘active’ witnessing and say out loud the patient’s full name, clinic number and D.O.B. This is to help reduce the risk of automaticity.

Witnessing must happen in ‘real time’ and ‘in person’ in order to maximise the chance of errors being identified at the time the procedure takes place. Witnessing cannot take place via video call. The witnessing record must be signed immediately following completion of the witnessing step.

The witnessing procedure must take place prior to moving any gametes/embryos into a new dish/tube.

Once a witnessing procedure is underway, the witness and operator must ignore all other distractions, including for example incoming phone calls. Other staff members must not disturb staff who are undertaking witnessing steps. Interrupting and returning to a task is a common source of error.

In cases where the patient’s identity is required, the patient must proactively confirm their own full name and D.O.B. If the patient is unable to speak the local language, consider arranging an independent interpreter to assist.



Tags must be attached to all pots/dishes/tubes containing gametes/embryos. The only exceptions are the test tubes that follicular fluid is collected into, the dish used for searching follicular fluid with during oocyte retrieval and dishes used for searching for sperm during SSR.

Tags already assigned to a particular patient must not be re-assigned to a different patient.

Dishes/tubes already labelled with a patient’s details must not be re-allocated to a different patient.

Critical workstations must not contain gametes or embryos belonging to more than one patient at any one time (unless a dual workstation is being used).

The samples must always remain within the RI Reader area to ensure samples from other patients can be detected.

When witnessing takes place, the following information must be recorded

• The witnessing step performed i.e. IVF Insemination

• The date and time of the witnessing check

• The signature of the person performing the procedure (or identity noted on RI Witness Manager)

• The signature of the witness (or identity noted on RI Witness Manager)

Discard of culture dishes/tubes containing gametes/embryos is a double witness step and a ‘discard’ step must be built into the witness map to record this procedure.

Discard of empty culture dishes/tubes does not require a double witness, however all dishes must be removed from the RI system prior to being disposed of by pressing the ‘discard’ button on RI Witness.

Regular audits must be undertaken to check practitioner compliance with the procedure.

##### 5.2 Electronic Witnessing Systems

Electronic witnessing systems can be used by clinics subject to appropriate risk assessment. Each clinic must have their own Witnessing Risk Assessment using template TFP1-FRM-H&S-0010.

RI Witness is utilised by all clinics within Oxford Medical Kuwait.

RI Witness utilises RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) technology to track patient’s samples as they enter the culture system and move from vessel to vessel.


Each dish/tube must be labelled with the patient’s full name, clinic number and D.O.B. This can be written by hand or an adhesive label can be used. In addition, an RFID witness tag must also be attached to each dish/tube. The RI Witness reader then determines whether the correct patient samples are within the reader area. If an incorrect sample is brought into the reader area, an audio/visual alarm is triggered.

Electronic witnessing systems such as this allow practitioners to work independently for the majority of laboratory processes. However, some steps within the laboratory must still be double witnessed by a second practitioner. These include:

###### Procuring gametes (collecting eggs or sperm, including SSR)

###### Mixing gametes either by IVF or ICSI

###### Embryo Transfer

###### PGT

###### Cryopreservation of gametes/embryos

###### Thawing/warming of gametes/embryos

###### IUI

###### Placing gametes or embryos into a cryostorage vessel

###### Disposing of gametes and embryos (this does not include disposal of empty dishes/tubes that have contained gametes/embryos)

###### Transporting gametes and embryos

######  gametes or embryos

###### Receiving gametes or embryos

###### RI Witness patient ID card assignment

-   **Admin assigning a witness step (this is considered an entry point)**

RI Witness will alert both visually and audibly in the event of an unassigned tag being used or a mismatch. In the event of a mismatch the procedure must be stopped immediately and investigated.

Each RI system is pre-programmed with a workflow map known as the ‘Witness Flow Diagram’. This allow RI Witness to determine which step is anticipated next in the treatment process.

##### 5.3 Witnessing Hardware

The following equipment is required:

###### RI Witness Dish Reader (preferably flush fitted into the flowhood surface)

###### RI Witness Card Reader for patient RI cards

###### Andrology Witness Reader for sperm preparations

###### Test Tube Witness Reader for inseminations

###### RFID Tags (large rectangular tag is for test tubes)

###### Patient ID Cards

###### RI Fob for logging into the RI System

All labels and tags must be firmly attached to the culture dishes to ensure they do not fall off.

An unassigned tag can be identified on the RI Witness system by the presence of a ‘?’. If an unassigned tag is removed from the witness area an audio sound is triggered. This acts as a prompt to check whether the dish has not been assigned in error.

Assigned tags will result in the patient’s full name, clinic number and D.O.B being displayed in the RI Witness System.

RI Witness labels can be used to label culture dishes and tubes. Using the RI Witness Manager Software, label sheets can be printed for patients and their partners on a variety of different shaped/sized labels. The labels show the patients full name, clinic number and D.O.B. The label sheet with a ‘white’ background provides greatest visibility.

Prior to commencing each treatment cycle, check the patients’ details are correct within RI Witness Manger, the relationship status is correct and displays the patient’s actual partner.

As a minimum the patient ID Card must be used at oocyte collection, embryo transfer and IUI. If SSR is performed ‘on site’ the patient ID card must also be used.

##### Prior to working within the RI Witness System, the operator must ‘login’. This can be performed by inputting a pre-set passcode or using a pre-assigned RI fob.

##### 5.4 RI Witness Workflow Diagram

The RI Witness system within each clinic is pre-programmed with a workflow diagram. The workflow diagram ensures that only the relevant next steps are shown as options to the operator during each procedure, these are called ‘forcing functions’. This helps guide staff and reduce errors.

The workflow diagram must encompass all witnessing steps within the laboratory and must be kept up to date to reflect current processes. It is the responsibility of the laboratory manager to ensure the Witness map is kept up date. Working outside of the witness flow diagram increases the risk of error.

It may be necessary on occasion to ‘Admin Assign’ an RI tag. As this process requires the operator to input the patients identifying details into RI Witness it is classified as an ‘entry point’ and a second witness is required.

##### 5.5 Mismatches, Witnessing Errors & Technical Failures

In the event of a mismatch the procedure must be stopped immediately and investigated. The procedure may only re-commence providing there is a clear explanation for the mismatch and this has been resolved.

An explanation for the mismatch must be entered into RI Witness.

The mismatches within the clinic must be evaluated as part of the annual witnessing audit.

In the event of a witnessing error, the cycle must be stopped immediately to prevent further harm. The laboratory manager must be informed, and a manager investigation must be undertaken. A non-conformity must be raised within the controlled register and the Kuwait MoH must be informed or other relevant regulatory body if applicable).

In the event of a technical failure, if a single workstation is affected, try to use an alternative workstation. If no other workstation is available or the total system has failed, revert to 100% manual witnessing. All witnessing steps would then require a second witness and all the witnessing steps must be recorded in the laboratory notes.

Report the technical fault to PGD Labs (Kuwait) or the local IT team as appropriate.

##### 5.6 Training

All staff undertaking witnessing procedures, even those acting solely as the second witness. must undertake witnessing training prior to undertaking witnessing procedures. The Oxford Medical Kuwait Witnessing Training workbook must be completed (TFP1-SCT-HR-0019) which includes:

-   Knowledge and understanding of the Oxford Medical Kuwait Witnessing SOP
-   Practical training in how to perform witnessing
-   Knowledge of actions to be taken in the event of a mismatch/witnessing error
-   Awareness of the witness flow diagram

Refresher training should be undertaken following any prolonged breaks from practice, flowing any witnessing incidents or following any changes to the witnessing system.

#### Monitoring

A Oxford Medical Kuwait laboratory manager is responsible for auditing this SOP at least once annually.

The audit must include a review of a sample of patients notes to check compliance to the SOP. It must also include a review of the signatory log, training records, witness map, mismatch log and direct observation of witnessing practice.
