## 1. Purpose

To define how adverse events are recognised, reported, contained, investigated by root-cause analysis (RCA), and prevented from recurring. This SOP operationalises the adverse-events policy (ELB-POL-005) and works alongside deviation handling (ELB-SOP-003) within the ISO 15189 quality management system. Its aim is to protect patients and their material, to learn from harm and near-harm, and to act without blame on the people involved.

## 2. Scope

Applies to any event that harmed, or could have harmed, a **patient couple's own gametes or embryos** (autologous only), or the patient, arising from laboratory activity. Includes loss, damage, misidentification, cross-contamination risk, equipment or cryostorage failure affecting stored material, and near misses. Excludes the clinical-outcome management owned by the treating service. Donor and surrogacy content is out of scope.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Any staff member** — recognises and immediately reports an adverse event or near miss; takes safe containment action first.
- **Laboratory Lead** — directs containment, secures evidence, and convenes the RCA.
- **Quality Manager** — manages the adverse-event report, facilitates RCA, and tracks resulting CAPA to closure (ELB-SOP-003).
- **Medical Director** — owns adverse-event review, decides on patient notification with the treating clinician, and authorises external reporting where required.
- **Treating clinician** — leads communication with the patient/couple.

## 4. Definitions & abbreviations

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Adverse event | An event causing actual harm or loss to a patient or their material |
| Near miss | An event that could have caused harm but did not, by chance or by a recovered check |
| Sentinel event | A serious adverse event (e.g. loss of stored material, misidentification) requiring immediate review |
| RCA | Root-cause analysis — structured identification of the underlying cause(s) |
| Just culture | An approach that distinguishes system failure from reckless behaviour and does not blame honest error |

## 5. Equipment, materials & media

Not applicable as a bench procedure. The tools are the adverse-event report (**ELB-LOG-025**), the deviation log (ELB-LOG-023), RCA worksheets, and the relevant procedural records.

## 6. Pre-procedure checks

1. Confirm immediate safety of any patient material involved and secure its identity (ELB-SOP-002).
2. Preserve evidence — do not discard failed equipment, suspect media, alarm logs or records.
3. Determine whether the event is sentinel (immediate Medical Director involvement) or non-sentinel.

> **CRITICAL — protect, preserve, then report.** Stabilise affected gametes/embryos and preserve the evidence (equipment, lots, logs, EW data) before reconstructing what happened.

## 7. Procedure

1. **Contain.** Take immediate action to limit harm — re-store material in compliant conditions, isolate failed equipment, or quarantine a suspect lot.
2. **Report.** Notify the Laboratory Lead and Quality Manager without delay. For a sentinel event, notify the Medical Director immediately.
3. **Open the record.** Complete the adverse-event report (ELB-LOG-025): description, time, people and material involved, immediate harm, and containment taken. Cross-link the deviation log entry (ELB-LOG-023).
4. **Convene RCA** for any actual harm, sentinel event, or recurrent near miss. Assemble those with knowledge of the process, working under a **just culture**.
5. **Reconstruct the timeline** from records, EW data, alarm/monitoring logs and interviews.
6. **Analyse cause** using a structured method (e.g. the "five whys" and contributing-factor mapping). Identify the underlying system cause(s), not only the proximate human action.
7. **Define CAPA.** Specify corrective and preventive actions with owners and due dates; route through deviation/CAPA handling (ELB-SOP-003).
8. **Decide notification.** With the Medical Director and treating clinician, agree patient/couple communication and any external/regulatory reporting required.
9. **Implement and verify** the actions; confirm effectiveness before closing the event.
10. **Close and share the learning** across the team, de-identified as appropriate.

## 8. Critical control points & witnessing

Any movement or re-storage of patient material during containment is witnessed (ELB-SOP-002).

| Checkpoint | What is verified | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **W1** | Identity of material moved, re-stored or quarantined during containment | EW match (RI Witness) or second-person check |

> **CRITICAL CONTROL POINT.** RCA examines the system, not the person. Identifying "who" without identifying "why the system allowed it" produces blame, not prevention. Reckless or wilful acts are handled separately and do not excuse a missing system safeguard.

## 9. Acceptance / rejection criteria

An adverse-event investigation is **complete** when the timeline is reconstructed, root cause(s) are identified, CAPA is implemented and verified effective, patient notification is decided, and the Medical Director has signed off. It is **not closed** while harm assessment, root cause, or required notification remains open.

## 10. Deviations

Failure to report a known adverse event is itself a serious nonconformity (ELB-POL-006, ELB-LOG-023) and is escalated to the Medical Director. The adverse-event pathway and the deviation/CAPA pathway run together: every adverse event is also recorded as a deviation, but not every deviation is an adverse event.

## 11. Records generated

- Adverse-event report — **ELB-LOG-025**.
- Linked deviation entry and CAPA — **ELB-LOG-023** (ELB-SOP-003).
- RCA worksheet and effectiveness-verification evidence.
- Patient-notification record (held with the clinical service).

## 12. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

The **requirement** to investigate, learn from and prevent adverse events is **guideline-anchored** (ISO 15189; ESHRE 2015; ASRM 2022). The local **sentinel-event definitions**, the **reporting timelines**, and the **escalation thresholds** are **locally defined** and recorded in **Appendix A (ELB-APP-A)** (flagged 🔧, requiring Medical Director sign-off and alignment with any licensing-authority reporting duty).

## 14. Teaching point

The most dangerous response to harm is to find a person to blame and stop there, because the system that let the error through is still in place for the next person. Root-cause analysis asks why the safeguards did not catch it — a tired embryologist, an ambiguous label, a missed alarm are symptoms of a process that can be made safer. Near misses are free lessons: treat the one that was caught with the same seriousness as the one that was not.
