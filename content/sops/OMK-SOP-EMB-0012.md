## Purpose

To describe the procedure for analysing semen parameters and carrying out freeze and thaw procedures for patient samples. Sperm cryopreservation can be used to preserve fertility or store sperm as back up prior to treatment.

## Scope

Applicable to all embryologists, andrologists and practitioners deemed competent to perform semen analysis and freezing:

Gamete/embryo donation and surrogacy are out of scope (prohibited in Kuwait).

## Definitions

PPE Personal Protective Equipment

IUI Intra Uterine Insemination

IVF In Vitro Fertilisation

ICSI Intra Cytoplasmic Sperm Injection

### 5.2 Equipment Checks

Prior to performing the sperm freeze procedure the equipment to be used must be checked to ensure it is operating optimally.

The flowhood must be checked to ensure the flow is on and the hood is clear of used pots/pipettes and tubes.

_Option 1: Charge Dry Shipper_. Fully charge the dry shipper with liquid nitrogen prior to freezing and ensure no liquid nitrogen remains in the bottom prior to freezing. The temperature of the dry shipper should then be checked using a cryo temperature probe and must be -170°C. Ensure appropriate PPE is used when handling liquid nitrogen (safety glasses or face visor, cryogloves and closed toe shoes)

_Option 2:Dilvac/cryobox._ Fill with with liquid nitrogen. Ensure appropriate PPE is used when handling liquid nitrogen (safety glasses or face visor, cryogloves & closed toe shoes).

Ensure that an appropriate dry shipper/dilvac/cryobox is used for freezing of the sample based on the patient’s infection screen results. Ensure the heat sealer is present and switched on.

### 5.3 Witnessing Checks (Full Name, Clinic Number & D.O.B.)

Check the required Kuwait MoH forms (or local country equivalent)have been completed providing consent to sperm freezing and that the viral infection screens are in date.

List of Consent Forms that must be completed prior to freezing in the UK:

1.  Kuwait MoH CD Consent
2.  Kuwait MoH GS Consent (if patient **does not** **have a partner**) **OR**

Kuwait MoH MT Consent (if patient **has a partner)**

1.  Oxford Medical Kuwait Freezing and Storage of Gametes Consent

Check the required screens have been completed and what the results are. The storage system must clearly separate samples that are unscreened/quarantined, negative and positive for the infections listed below.

The following viral screens must have been completed within 3 months of freezing:

1\. HIV 1 and 2: Anti-HIV 1,2

2\. Hepatitis B: HBsAG and Anti-HBc

3\. Hepatitis C: Anti-HCV-Ab

Ask the patient to state their full name and D.O.B. Check this information along with their clinic number matches the patient’s laboratory notes and sample pot. Sign for these checks in the laboratory record/sample production form.

Hand the patient the sample pot and provide the patient with sample production instructions. Ask the patient to initial the sample pot/sign the production form.

Record the number of day’s abstinence and any illness/medication in last 2-3 months in not recorded on the sample production form. Instruct the patient to inform the laboratory team if not all the sample is collected.

If the sample has been produced off site, on arrival at the clinic ask the patient to state their full name and D.O.B. Check this information along with their clinic number matches the patient’s laboratory record and sample pot. Sign for these checks in the laboratory record/sample production form. Ensure the patient has completed the off-site production declaration form.

Once the sample has been produced, transfer the sample to the flowhood.

Only one sample may be processed in a flowhood at any one time.

Samples that are incorrectly labelled, unlabelled or whereby there is doubt as to its identification must be rejected. If the name and D.O.B. on the sample pot and production form do not match the sample must be rejected. If the initials on the sample pot and the sample pot label do not match the sample must be rejected.

Alongside a second witness check the full name, D.O.B. and clinic number on the sample pot and any test tubes for freezing match the laboratory notes and that on the RI Witness system.

Alongside a second witness check the CBS straws and visi tubes/cassettes are labelled with patient’s full name, clinic number, D.O.B. and date of freeze and that they match with that on the laboratory notes and RI witness system. RI barcodes may also be attached to the straws.

Alongside a second witness, check the location of the samples in the dewar matches that in the laboratory notes.

### 5.4 Sample Production

Semen samples should where possible be produced on site. If the patient is unable to produce on site, offer the patient the option to produce off site. Provide the patient with a labelled sample pot (full name, clinic number and D.O.B.) If the sample is produced off-site the patient must sign an off-site production declaration form stating date/time/place of production and that the sample is his own and has not been tampered with. Advise the patient to keep the sample warm and to bring it to the clinic within 50 minutes of production.

Patients may also be given the option of producing the sample into a non-toxic condom.

If the patient is to produce on site, the sample production room must be cleaned prior to production, by wiping all surfaces (including door handles, the sample production hatch, chairs, pens etc.) in the production room using alcohol free disinfectant wipes/Oosafe.

Check with the patient their abstinence period, any recent illnesses or medications and if they have been abroad recently to assess Zika/Ebola risk.

All patients must be triaged for Covid-19 before attending the clinic.

### 5.5 Freezing in Vapour Procedure

Straws need to be frozen in vapour prior to placing in liquid nitrogen. This can be done via either of the two methods described:

_Option 1:_ Place straws in cryo cassette/visi & Cane in charged dry shipper for a minimum of 30 minutes.

_Option 2:_ Suspend straws in cryo cassettes/visi tubes and canes above liquid nitrogen in a dilvac/cryobox or dewar for a minimum of 30 minutes.

### 5.6 Timing of Freeze Procedure & Sample Suitability

Begin freezing of the semen sample once liquefied and within 15-60 minutes post-production or once sample has been processed (for SSR or retrograde samples).

If the sample is viscous or fails to liquefy, GIVF+ can be added to the sample or the sample can be syringed/warmed to try and facilitate the process.

Samples are assessed via semen analysis prior to freezing.

Samples where motile sperm (motility grades A-C) are observed in the initial/post preparation sample are considered suitable for freezing.

Samples with a concentration of <1M/ml should be concentrated prior to freezing via centrifugation at 0.3-0.8 r.c.f.

SSR and retrograde samples must be processed prior to freezing and the prepared stock sample frozen. Refer to Oxford Medical Kuwait Sperm Preparation for Treatment for the preparation procedure.

SSR samples may need to be assessed in a ‘lake’ dish prior to freezing.

**5.7 Cryoprotectant Exposure Times**

Vitrolife Sperm freeze solution is used to cryopreserve sperm samples.

Allow the cryoprotectant to warm to room temperature prior to use.

Expose the sample to cryoprotectant for 10 minutes at room temperature prior to placing in vapour.

**5.8 Preparation for the Procedure**

Remove the sperm freeze from the fridge and allow to warm to room temperature.

Label sufficient CBS straws with printed labels for the sample to be frozen, based on initial sample parameters and sample volume, with patient’s full name, clinic number, D.O.B. and freeze date.

Identify a location for the samples within the cryostore based on the patient’s infection screen results. Unscreened/quarantined, negative and positive samples must be stored separately. Split fertility preservation patient’s samples between a minimum of two dewars.

Enter the storage location(s) in IDEAs. Prepare a storage card if required

**5.9 Centrifugation Speeds and Timings**

Samples may be concentrated down to smaller volumes at 0.3-0.8 rcf for 5 minutes depending on initial sample concentration.

**5.10 Semen Analysis**

Assess the volume, concentration and motility of the initial semen sample.

Assessments of anti-sperm antibodies, pH, morphology, viability and round cells are all optional depending on the patient’s clinical history or sample characteristics.

The initial parameters of the sample are used to determine number of straws to be frozen and suitability for freezing.

Post-thaw, assess the motility of the sample.

#### 5.10.1 Assessment of Sample Volume (Reference Value ≥1.4ml)

Using a serological pipette, thoroughly mix the sample by pipetting up and down several times and then measure the total volume of the sample using the graduations on the pipette.

#### 5.10.2 Assessment of Concentration (Reference Value ≥16M/ml)

Mix 10µl of neat semen with 190µl of sterile water in an Eppendorf tube. Vortex to mix thoroughly. Attach the cover slip to the haemocytometer until Newtons Rings are visible. Gently pipette approximately 8-10 µl of the diluted sample onto each side of the haemocytometer chamber. Allow the chamber to settle for 4 minutes in a humid chamber before analysis.

Observe the chamber at X200-400 magnification. The chamber consists of two grids with 25 large squares on each. Depending on the concentration of the sperm in the sample, count either the number of whole sperm in 5 large squares per grid or 25 large squares per grid. Determine the concentration of the sample in M/ml. Determine the concentration of the sample in M/ml by dividing number counted by 2 (if 5 large squares counted on each side) or dividing the number counted by 10 (if 25 large squares counted on each side).

For samples where concentration assessment not possible, (<0.5M/ml) assess number of motile sperm seen on 10µl slide and report finding as the number of motile sperm per high power field.

#### 5.10.3 Assessment of Motility (Reference Value A + B= ≥30%)

Place 10µl of semen onto a clear glass slide and cover with a glass cover slip. Observe the slide at X200-400 magnification. Grade the motility of 200 sperm using the following criteria:

**Grade A** – rapid progressive motility

**Grade B** – slow or sluggish motility

**Grade C** – non-progressive motility

**Grade D** – immotile

Note the results as a percentage of each grade.

#### 5.10.4 Assessment of Morphology, if indicated (Reference Value ≥4%)

Place 5µl of semen onto a pre-stained morphology slide. Allow the slide to air dry for 15-30 minutes. Observe the slide at X1000 magnification under oil immersion. Grade 200 whole sperm as ‘normal’ or ‘abnormal’ according to WHO 2010 criteria. Note the result as a percentage of normal forms.

For sperm to be considered normal both the tail and head must be normal. All borderline sperm must be considered abnormal.

Normal sperm have smooth, oval shaped head with a well-defined acrosomal area comprising 40-70% of the head area. The acrosomal area should not contain any large vacuoles and not more than two small vacuoles. There should be no vacuoles in the post acrosomal region.

The midpiece should be slender and regular and approximately the same length as the head. The axis of the midpiece should be aligned with the axis of the sperm head. Cytoplasmic droplets should be < 1/3 of the size of the sperm head.

The tail should be regular and thinner than the midpiece and around ten times the length of the head.

#### 5.10.5 Assessment of Anti-Sperm Antibodies IgG, if indicated (Reference Value <50%)

Allow the reagents to warm to room temperature.

Thoroughly mix the reagent(s). Add 5µl of each reagent to a clear glass slide along with 5µl of semen. Mix on the slide using the pipette tip. Place a cover slip on top.

Allow the sample to settle for 3 minutes then observe the slide under X200-400 magnification.

Assess 100 motile sperm for the presence of beads bound to them. Convert the number of sperm showing evidence of binding to a percentage.

If no binding is seen on the initial assessment, re-assess after 10 minutes.

#### 5.10.6 Assessment of pH, if indicated (Reference pH ≥7.2)

Perform the pH assessment within 1 hour of sample production.

Place a droplet of neat semen onto pH paper/strip. Wait for the colour of the impregnated zone to become uniform. Compare the colour to the calibration strip and read the pH.

#### 5.10.7 Assessment of Viability, if indicated (Reference Value ≥54%)

SpermMobil can be used to initiate motility in viable non-motile sperm. The process for using SpermMobil is outlined in the Oxford Medical Kuwait ICSI SOP.

Alternatively, the Hypo-Osmotic Swelling Test (HOS) may be used to distinguish between viable and non-viable non-motile sperm.

#### 5.10.8 Assessment of Round Cells if indicated (quantify if concentration appears >5M/ml)

An assessment of round cells in the sample can be performed in the same way as a concentration assessment as described above.

If the concentration of round cells is >5M/ml, determine the number of leukocytes in the sample using Leucostain Plus (See Diagnostic Semen Analysis & Post Vasectomy Analysis SOP).

**5.11 Freezing Procedure**

Using a serological pipette measure the volume of the semen sample.

Transfer an equal volume of equilibrated sperm freeze solution into a conical tube.

Using a glass pipette add the cryoprotectant to the semen sample dropwise, tilt/swirl the pot or tube after each drop of cryoprotectant is added in order to mix.

Once all the cryoprotectant has been added, pipette the sample up/down a minimum of five times to ensure the sample and cryoprotectant are thoroughly mixed. Take care not to create bubbles.

Expose the sample to cryoprotectant for 10 minutes at room temperature prior to placing in vapour.

During the 10 minutes exposure time transfer the sample into CBS straws using an adapted syringe and filling nozzle, leaving a 1cm air gap at the end of the straw.

Seal the CBS straw with the heat sealer.

Repeat until all straws are filled. Allocate a small volume to be used for a test thaw in a separate labelled straw labelled as ‘test thaw’.

Place the straws into a labelled cryocassette/visi and cane, and transfer into vapour for a minimum of 30 minutes.

After 30 minutes in vapour, transfer the straws into the designated storage location in the nitrogen tank alongside a second witness. Both operator and witness must sign for these checks.

Perform test thaw by thawing out test thaw straw to room temperature and assessing motility on the thawed sample.

Poor samples may need to be assessed in a ‘lake dish’. The process for assessing samples in a lake is outlined in the Oxford Medical Kuwait ICSI SOP.

Clear the flowhood of any used consumables. Use Oosafe spray ± sterile water to clean the flowhood.

Complete the laboratory and witnessing records.

Enter the results of the semen analysis and freeze into Ideas, including any comments from the laboratory team about sample suitability.

**5.12 Thawing Patient Sperm for Use in Treatment**

Before beginning the thawing process perform local checks to ensure that the thaw is being conducted on the correct date and oocytes have been successfully retrieved at TVOR or an IUI is scheduled. If ICSI is the planned treatment and <3 oocytes have been collected, denude the oocytes prior to thawing the sperm in order to check that there is an oocyte(s) suitable for injection.

Perform the witnessing checks to ensure the correct sample has been selected for thawing. Determine the number of straws/ampoules to thaw based on initial parameters and intended treatment. If more than one ejaculate has been frozen, identify the correct ejaculate to be thawed.

Sperm thawing requires a second witness to be used.

Check the patient’s full name, clinic number, D.O.B. and freeze date on the visi/cassette, ampoules/straws match that in the laboratory paperwork.

Check the patient’s full name, clinic number, D.O.B. on the test tubes for preparation match that in the laboratory notes and RI witness. Both operator and witness must sign for these checks.

Allow the ampoules/straws to warm to room temperature.

Using the straw cutter, release the contents of the straw into a labelled tube.

Using a serological pipette measure the volume of the thawed sample.

Add an equivalent volume of equilibrated GIVF+ to the thawed sample dropwise, tilting the tube after each drop. GIVF+ must be added slowly in order to gradually adjust the osmolality of the sample.

## Remove 50µl of the post thaw sample and place in an Eppendorf tube for subsequent concentration and motility assessment.

## Assess the initial concentration and motility of the thawed sample.

## The thawed sample can then be prepared for treatment using a method suitable for the sample’s concentration and motility. The process for sperm preparation is outlined in Oxford Medical Kuwait Sperm Preparation for Treatment.

## Clear the flowhood of any used consumables. Use Oosafe spray ± sterile water to clean the flowhood.
