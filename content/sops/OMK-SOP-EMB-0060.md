## 1. Purpose

To define the overarching framework for laboratory quality control (QC) and equipment maintenance: how setpoints are monitored, how instruments are calibrated and serviced, how out-of-range results are acted on, and how the QC record demonstrates that the environment for gametes and embryos is fit at all times. This SOP is the umbrella; the device-specific procedures (incubators, gas analysers, thermometers, pH meters, LN₂ tanks, air quality) sit beneath it.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all equipment and environmental parameters that affect the safety and development of the **patient couple's own gametes and embryos** (autologous only) — incubators, heated surfaces, gas supply, cryostorage, microscopes, micromanipulators, measuring instruments and laboratory air. Covers routine QC checks, calibration, preventive maintenance and corrective repair. Detailed methods are in the referenced device SOPs. Donor and surrogacy content is out of scope.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **All laboratory staff** — perform assigned QC checks, record results, and report any out-of-range result immediately.
- **Equipment custodian / Laboratory Lead** — maintains the equipment inventory, schedules calibration and preventive maintenance, and authorises return to service after repair.
- **Quality Manager** — owns the QC plan, reviews QC trends, and ensures calibration traceability and records.
- **Medical Director** — approves the QC plan and the locally defined check frequencies, ranges and alert limits (**Appendix A**).
- **Service provider** — performs contracted calibration/servicing to the agreed specification.

## 4. Definitions & abbreviations

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| QC check | A scheduled verification that a parameter is within its acceptable range |
| Calibration | Comparison of an instrument against a traceable reference and adjustment as needed |
| Preventive maintenance (PM) | Scheduled servicing to keep equipment within specification |
| Out-of-range / OOR | A result outside the defined acceptable range or alert limit |
| Traceability | An unbroken chain of calibration to a recognised reference standard |

## 5. Equipment, materials & media

- Monitored equipment: CO₂/tri-gas incubators, heated stages/blocks, microscopes and micromanipulators, gas analyser, thermometers, pH meter, LN₂ storage tanks, and air-handling/cleanroom systems.
- Calibrated reference instruments and certified standards (e.g. reference thermometer, pH buffers, gas standards) with valid certificates.
- Continuous monitoring and alarm systems where fitted (e.g. temperature, CO₂, LN₂ level).

> **IFU cross-reference.** Each instrument is operated, calibrated and maintained per its manufacturer IFU and the device-specific SOP. Calibration intervals and tolerances follow Appendix A and the IFU; do not extend an interval or relax a tolerance without Medical Director approval.

## 6. Pre-procedure checks

1. Confirm the QC schedule for the day and that the previous period's checks are complete and in range.
2. Confirm reference instruments/standards are within their own calibration validity.
3. Confirm alarm systems are active and have not been silenced.

> **CRITICAL — no work in an unverified environment.** Do not place or hold patient material in an incubator, on a heated surface, or in a tank whose QC is out of range or overdue until it is verified or the material is moved to a compliant unit.

## 7. Procedure

1. **Routine QC.** Perform scheduled checks at the defined frequency (**Appendix A** 🔧) per the device SOP — e.g. incubator temperature and CO₂ (OMK-SOP-EMB-0062), general temperature monitoring (OMK-SOP-EMB-0061), LN₂ levels, air quality.
2. **Record.** Enter each result with date, time, instrument ID and operator on the relevant QC log (e.g. OMK-LOG-EMB-0061 incubator QC; OMK-LOG-EMB-0063 calibration).
3. **Compare to range.** Judge each result against its acceptable range / alert limit (**Appendix A**). An in-range result is signed off; an OOR result triggers Section 9.
4. **Calibration.** Calibrate measuring instruments at the defined interval against traceable standards; record the as-found and as-left values and retain certificates (OMK-LOG-EMB-0063).
5. **Preventive maintenance.** Carry out or arrange scheduled PM; record what was done and the next due date.
6. **Trend review.** The Quality Manager reviews QC trends to detect drift before it becomes a failure.
7. **Corrective repair.** On failure, remove the unit from use, label it clearly, arrange repair, and re-verify against specification before return to service.

## 8. Critical control points & witnessing

QC checks themselves do not handle patient gametes; where an OOR result requires moving patient material to another unit, the identity witness checkpoints apply (OMK-SOP-EMB-0002).

| Checkpoint | What is verified | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **W1** | Identity of any patient material relocated because of an equipment failure | EW match (RI Witness) or second-person check |

> **CRITICAL CONTROL POINT.** An alarm or OOR reading is acted on, not silenced. Protect the material first, then diagnose the equipment.

## 9. Acceptance / rejection criteria

Equipment is **fit for use** when its current QC is in range and its calibration/PM is within validity. It is **rejected (removed from service)** when a result is OOR and cannot be immediately corrected, when calibration/PM is overdue, or when an alarm condition is unresolved. Out-of-range results are recorded and managed as deviations.

## 10. Deviations

Any OOR result, overdue calibration/PM, or equipment failure is managed under the deviation and CAPA policy (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005) and logged in the deviation log (OMK-SOP-EMB-0005). Where an environmental excursion may have affected patient material, the adverse-event pathway is opened (OMK-POL-EMB-0004).

## 11. Records generated

- Incubator QC log — **OMK-LOG-EMB-0061**.
- Calibration log with certificates — **OMK-LOG-EMB-0063**.
- Device-specific QC logs referenced by their SOPs (gas analyser, LN₂, air quality, temperature).
- Equipment maintenance/repair records and the equipment inventory.

## 12. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

The **requirement** for monitored setpoints, traceable calibration and planned maintenance is **guideline-anchored** (ISO 15189; ESHRE 2015; ASRM 2022). The **check frequencies**, **acceptable ranges and alert limits**, and **calibration/PM intervals** are **locally defined** and recorded in **Appendix A (OMK-APP-EMB-A)** (flagged 🔧, requiring Medical Director sign-off). Guideline-fixed targets (e.g. 37.0 °C culture temperature, pH 7.2–7.4) are recorded as guideline-anchored in Appendix A.

## 14. Teaching point

Embryos cannot tell you the incubator drifted; the QC record is the only voice they have. Quality control is not a set of readings to fill in — it is the standing proof that the conditions you assume were true actually were. Trend the numbers, not just the day's value: most equipment failures announce themselves as drift before they announce themselves as an alarm.
