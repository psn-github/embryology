## 1. Purpose

To define how chemicals are identified, labelled, stored, used and disposed of in the Embryology and Andrology Laboratory, so that staff are protected from chemical hazards and the culture environment is protected from chemical (VOC) contamination. Chemical hygiene serves two ends here that ordinary labs do not face together: worker safety and embryo safety.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all chemicals held or used in the laboratory: disinfectants, fixatives, solvents, reagents, cryoprotectant constituents and cleaning agents. Applies to autologous workflows only; donor and surrogacy work is not performed.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **All staff** — read the relevant SDS before first use, label and store chemicals correctly, and use the specified controls and PPE.
- **Laboratory Lead** — maintains the chemical inventory and the SDS file, and approves new chemicals for use in the air-controlled space.
- **Quality Manager** — audits labelling, storage and SDS currency, and reviews any exposure or spill.
- **Medical Director** — owns chemical-safety policy through ELB-POL-001/002.

## 4. Definitions & abbreviations

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| GHS | Globally Harmonised System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals |
| SDS | Safety Data Sheet (16-section GHS format) |
| Pictogram | GHS hazard symbol on the label and SDS |
| VOC | Volatile organic compound (embryo-relevant emission) |
| PPE | Personal protective equipment |

## 5. Equipment, materials & media

- GHS-labelled chemical containers; secondary containment for incompatibles.
- SDS file (current, accessible) for every chemical held.
- Fume extraction / ventilation for volatile chemicals; spill kit.
- PPE per the SDS (gloves — non-latex, ELB-POL-003; eye protection; etc.).

> **IFU / labelling cross-reference.** Every chemical bears a **GHS** label (product identifier, pictograms, signal word, hazard and precautionary statements) and has a current **SDS** on file. Decanted/working solutions are GHS-labelled with identity and hazard. Concentrations and contact times for embryo-safe disinfectants follow **Appendix A** and the IFU.

## 6. Pre-procedure checks

1. Confirm the chemical is GHS-labelled and the current SDS is on file and read.
2. Confirm the required controls (ventilation/fume extraction) and PPE are available and working.
3. Confirm use will not raise lab VOCs beyond control (coordinate with ELB-SOP-013); use volatile agents away from open culture.

## 7. Procedure

1. Obtain only the quantity needed; keep stocks minimal in the lab.
2. Store chemicals by GHS compatibility group, segregating incompatibles, away from heat/ignition and from culture areas.
3. Use volatile or hazardous chemicals under extraction, with the PPE specified by the SDS.
4. Label every working/decant container with GHS identity and hazard immediately on filling.
5. Wipe up minor spills promptly with the spill kit per the SDS; ventilate; for larger spills follow ELB-POL-001 and evacuate if directed.
6. Dispose of chemical waste by the route in **Appendix A** 🔧 / the SDS; do not pour incompatibles together (segregate from biohazardous waste, ELB-SOP-015).
7. After use, return chemicals to compliant storage and confirm closures are secure.

## 8. Critical control points & witnessing

This SOP is a safety/environmental control and does not generate gamete-identity events, so no per-step gamete witnessing applies. Introduction of any new chemical to the air-controlled space is reviewed and approved by the Laboratory Lead for embryo (VOC) safety before use.

> **CRITICAL CONTROL POINT.** No chemical is used in the laboratory without a GHS label and a current SDS. An unlabelled or unidentified container is quarantined, not used, and not poured to waste until identified.

## 9. Acceptance / rejection criteria

A chemical is acceptable for use when it is GHS-labelled, has a current SDS, is stored compatibly and is approved for the culture environment where relevant. Unlabelled, expired, incompatible-stored or unapproved chemicals are reject conditions.

## 10. Deviations

Spills, exposures, mislabelling and unapproved-chemical use are handled under the exposure-control and hazard-communication policies (ELB-POL-001, ELB-POL-002) and the deviation/CAPA policy (ELB-POL-006), and are recorded in the deviation log (ELB-LOG-023). Personal exposures follow ELB-POL-001.

## 11. Records generated

- Chemical inventory and SDS file (maintained by the Laboratory Lead).
- Spill/exposure entries — per ELB-POL-001 and **ELB-LOG-023**.
- VOC impact entries where relevant — **ELB-LOG-022**.

## 12. Guideline-linked & locally-defined values

Embryo-safe disinfectant concentrations and contact times 🔧, chemical-storage segregation map 🔧 and chemical-waste routes 🔧 are recorded in **Appendix A**. Labelling and SDS requirements are **guideline-anchored to GHS**; management aligns with ISO 15189. (OSHA does not apply and is not cited.)

## 13. References

- GHS — Globally Harmonised System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals (labelling and SDS).
- ISO 15189 — requirements for quality and competence in medical laboratories (safety, facilities).

## 14. Teaching point

In an IVF laboratory chemical hygiene protects two patients at once: the staff member who reads the SDS and the embryo that breathes the air. A volatile disinfectant used carelessly is both an exposure hazard and a VOC source. Label by GHS, store by compatibility, and always ask "what does this off-gas into the culture room?" before you open the bottle.
